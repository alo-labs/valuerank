## Objective and claim scope

AA's Pareto frontier of Intelligence Index vs. total USD cost to run its Intelligence Index does not answer the question ValueRank exists for: **which frontier AI model is actually lowest cost for the value it delivers in practice**, once (a) subscription-plan economics are considered and (b) benchmarks geared to real software-engineering work are weighed alongside general capability. ValueRank serves two buyers, ranked separately: **API buyers** (pay per token, incl. third-party agents) and **Plan buyers** (provider subscription apps/CLIs: Claude Code, Codex, ChatGPT, SuperGrok…).

Claim scope: "value among the ranked configurations (AA-selected frontier and near-frontier configurations, plus configurations retained for exact DeepSWE and Bug Hunt evidence; see Cohort), under the published benchmark mix and evidence". Every ranked row is a **configuration** (model version, effort, fallback, route), with a compact configuration ID and an expandable per-leaf execution manifest; tables and claims say "configuration", not "model" (E78). Not a market-wide optimum across all configurations and workloads.

## Outputs

1. **Headline ranking** per basis by value score = 0.25 × cost score + 0.75 × quality score (owner decisions O2, cost weight 25%). There is **no "recommended" label** (O22). Today the Plan ranking is displayed arithmetic on owner-named transfers and unidentified observed multiples, labelled exploratory: no Plan frontier or bar claim is supported (E61, E67). Uncertainty is shown beside each rank as per-stratum scenario shares (Uncertainty); each share table states that it excludes benchmark sampling noise beyond S1M and fitted-rating uncertainty (E155). Two named quantities are printed per basis: (a) the **anchor exchange rate** — in the unclipped region a cost doubling changes value by −100·β·ln2 / ln(c_H/c_L) points and is offset by +100·β·ln2 / ((1−β)·ln(c_H/c_L)) quality points, β = 0.25, from the active anchor version; (b) the **within-cohort influence** 0.25·SD(S_C) vs 0.75·SD(S_Q), where S_C is the cost score and S_Q the displayed quality Q̄_i, computed on the declared **common-workload panel** (E91): the rows scored on the main workload W with its anchor object, the same rows for both SDs, with panel size and excluded rows shown beside it. **Dispersion convention (E180)**: every cohort SD and variance in this methodology (within-cohort influence, the non-discriminating threshold, drift spreads, covariance allocation) is the population statistic over the declared panel (divisor n), because the panel is the population being described, not a sample from a larger one; a panel of fewer than two rows returns "undefined (insufficient panel)"; scenario dispersion (E87) is likewise the population variance over the stream's scenarios. Subset-workload strata get their own statistic (undefined below two rows); a statistic over the whole displayed table mixes anchor objects and may be shown only as "displayed-table dispersion"; a zero denominator returns "undefined — no composite dispersion". A release is flagged (not held) when cost's share of the common-workload influence, 0.25·SD(S_C) / (0.25·SD(S_C) + 0.75·SD(S_Q)), is under 10% ("cost not discriminating") or above 90% ("quality not discriminating", E126); the exchange rate of every anchor version is published as a series, and a trimmed-anchor diagnostic (10th and 90th percentile reference costs with the same ln 2 padding) reports rank shifts only, never replacing the primary anchors; the flag is informational and β never changes (owner decision O2). Because cost and quality covary across the cohort, the SD ratio describes dispersion, not contribution, so the same panel also publishes the **value-variance allocation (E189)** Cov(0.25·S_C, V)/Var(V) and Cov(0.75·Q, V)/Var(V), which sum to 1, and the τ_b of the cost-only and quality-only orderings against the value ordering (informational; the flag is unchanged). **Nominal weight beside influence (E196)**: the headline calls the 25% cost weight nominal and prints cost's value-variance allocation for each basis beside it (generated each release; illustration from 2026-10-21: "25% cost score (nominal weight; 37% of API and 51% of Plan value variance)"); the anchors are not recalibrated to equalise influence, because the weight is owner policy (2026-10-19) and the anchors are frozen evidence ranges, and the disclosure never changes a score. Cost-weight slider results appear beside the main table as sensitivity columns; the headline ranks always use the 25% weight. No quality floor on a score (O8); ranking itself requires the full benchmark set (O36, O38), which supersedes O8's ranking of partially covered rows, so no ranked row is provisional today. **Basket version in the headline (E59)**: the headline names the active basket version and its components (today "SDLC basket v1: 7 AA Index SDLC evaluations + Coding Agent Index; pending FrontierCode, ITBench-AA"), and the target basket appears in the readiness table. **Cost-weight disclosure (E60)**: the anchor exchange rate (quality points that offset a cost doubling) and the within-cohort cost influence are printed beside the first table, with a sensitivity curve of the exchange rate over c_H/c_L.

## Source contract (versioned registry)

**Evidence bundle (E159)**: each release is (method version, registry, evidence bundle, extractor versions, outputs). The bundle lists every captured artifact the build reads — source payloads or permitted captures, committed with the release — with its content hash, capture time and extractor, plus a bundle hash; every scored observation records its artifact, field locator and published value. Rebuilding from the bundle at the pinned publication clock must reproduce the admitted components, route selection, anchors, scores and deterministic interval statuses without consulting live pages (and the scenario outputs under the recorded RNG, seed and draw order); where redistribution is restricted, the permitted capture is retained and its hash and locator published. Two cases are kept apart. Overlapping circles off the line are separated at capture by hovering each and recording the resolution, as the 2026-10-17 capture did for GLM-5.2 and GPT-5.5. Two circles at one vertex (an exact cost-and-Index tie) are separated the same way; if hovering cannot separate them, the vertex check fails and the build stops until the owner records a decision. A joint scenario simulation (N = 10,000) is the single engine. **Primitive sampling (E83)**: scenarios draw the primitive variables of the constrained evidence model (E73) under the selected branch and then derive costs, capacities, multiples, quality and value; derived quantities (e.g. Plan cost = C_W / M_W = F / λ under compatible identified transport) are never drawn independently; every scenario must pass the applicable identities and feasibility checks; infeasible combinations are never silently rejected and redrawn unless that conditional sampling law is itself declared; where public evidence supports no joint sampling mechanism, the branch publishes bounds or is unevaluable (fixtures: C/M = F/λ; gross cash changing with consumption under an evidenced payment rule; shared donor and price primitives propagating to every affected row; individually admissible draws that violate a joint constraint). **Cost is drawn too**: published rounding supports reporting-precision draws, and public paired per-task or per-run cost observations for the exact configuration support resampling at their evidenced level; an analyst-selected token-use range belongs only in a named S2 stress branch, and without supporting observations repeatability is reported as "not quantified", never zero and never an invented S0 distribution (E134; today S0 cost draws are reporting precision only, and the build rejects an S0 primitive whose parameter_derivation cites only an analyst convention). **Joint rounding law (E154)**: rounding is a conventional treatment, not an empirical error distribution. The build compiles each connected constraint component of rounded published records (records linked by an accounting identity, e.g. rounded component costs and a rounded aggregate) and gives it a registered joint-law object (constraint_component_id, published field IDs, free source coordinates, joint_law_id, sampler_version, sampling_status). A singleton component keeps its independent uniform kernel (arcsine alternative); a derived quantity is the identity applied to its drawn primitives — a basket cost is its exact components plus each rounded component's own draw, never one uniform on the summed interval; an affine-linked component uses the registered v1 convention, uniform volume on the feasible polytope in its declared free coordinates, sampled only by an explicitly registered exact algorithm within its resource limit; without a registered law, or beyond that limit, the component keeps deterministic bounds and the affected frequencies are withheld as "joint rounding law not evaluated" — feasibility alone never activates sampling (fixture: two rounded components and a rounded aggregate, every draw inside all three reporting intervals and satisfying the identity; equivalent accounting representations give the same derived-cost distribution). ## Normative definitions (executable specification)

- **Coverage and scores.** Leaf weights w_b are percent points summing to 100, and z and u are on 0–100. p_i = Σ_{b∈O_i} w_b / 100. Displayed observed-only quality Q̄_i = Σ_{b∈O_i} w_b z_ib / (100·p_i). Full-composite score Q_i(θ) = (Σ_{b∈O_i} w_b z_ib(θ) + Σ_{b∉O_i} w_b u_ib) / 100, u_ib ∈ [0,100] (fixture: DeepSWE 30 points missing, every other leaf at 60 → p_i = 0.70, Q̄_i = 60, Q_i ∈ [42, 72]). **Formal bounds** (E35): Q_i^L = min_{θ∈Θ} Q_i(θ) and Q_i^U = max_{θ∈Θ} Q_i(θ) over the joint admissible set Θ below (measurement intervals of observed leaves and u_ib ∈ [0,100] for missing leaves); "missing at 0/100 at the point estimate" is not the bound. S0 uses Q̄_i; S1/S2 use the full-composite equation. Any sampling law for wholly missing leaves is a declared scenario convention (with one published alternative), never evidence; formal bounds are distribution-free.
- **Joint admissible set Θ (for interval outputs: frontier and bar).** Fixed: nominal weights, primary price rule, frozen route, linear GDPval map (E1). **Target (owner decision O25)**: interval outputs protect the **published record** — the benchmark results and costs as published for each exact configuration (for cost: the published deployment-equivalent cost of the fixed workload). They are robust to the record's reporting precision and to missing evidence, not to run-to-run noise. Benchmark repeatability and fitted-parameter uncertainty (published confidence intervals, run-to-run ranges) are outside the S0–S2 shares and are reported only by separately named streams whose documented estimand supports them (E145, E155; in v1, S1M as a conventional range sensitivity inside Terminal-Bench's published interval, E162, E165); token-use and price-basis variation enter only named S2 specification branches. **Uncertainty descriptions (E155)** are generated from the stream manifests and printed beside every uncertainty output (v1: S0 "published-record reporting-precision scenarios"; S1 "S0 plus conventional missing-evidence scenarios"; S2 core "S1 plus hierarchical weight stress"; S1M "S1 plus conventional range sensitivity inside Terminal-Bench's published 95% confidence interval"; other benchmark repeatability and fitted-parameter uncertainty "not quantified in v1 scenario shares"); a release-text lint rejects any statement that a source interval or run range enters scenario shares unless an active stream's allowlist admits that target. Variable in Θ: rounding intervals of published values at their published precision, missing-leaf and missing-component domains (identification), identified M_W ranges and other structural bounds (e.g. deployment-policy bias bounds). **Constraint basis (O19 under O25)**: each uncertainty object records constraint_basis (rounding of the record / published interval / public repeated runs / structural bound / analyst assumption / score domain), estimand, randomness_unit, constraint_target, coverage_claim and supporting_public_evidence (E46), and separates observed_statistic from target_parameter. **Target-compatibility gate (E49)**: an interval narrows a Θ variable only when its documented constraint_target is that published-record variable (or a public, justified mapping connects them); published confidence intervals and run ranges target other estimands and therefore never enter Θ; a full score domain expresses the absence of a narrower admissible constraint on a missing value. Analyst-assumed allowances never enter Θ. **Completeness (E11)**: every input, whatever its weight, enters Θ through a documented constraint or its full domain; an input with neither makes the affected output "unevaluable" for that row. Missing repeatability evidence never becomes zero uncertainty. Published benchmark intervals are consumed with their stated interpretation. Shared constraints (prices, donor measurements, task/run observations, quota pools) move jointly. With transfers off, a Plan route that depends entirely on an owner-authorised transfer is "unevaluable" in interval outputs (never silently replaced by API cost). Tests: widening Θ cannot move a row from "possible" to "definite" or create a confirmed result; evidence loss is tested in two stages (E38) — the result within R_W may change when a competitor leaves R_W, but the selected-cohort extension can never strengthen.
- **Plan measurement vs transport.** M_observed = eligible API-valued measured consumption ÷ gross cash paid (the measured workload). **M_W** ∈ [L, U] = the multiple for the scored workload W (O15 basket), bound to W's hash, the release price-list version and the quota-schema version; transport evidence for another workload qualifies only if its capacity model or conservative bound also covers W, with its identification mechanism recorded: direct compatible-workload measurement / publicly evidenced additive capacity model / evidenced transport bound / unidentified. - **Interval decision procedure (E40, amended by E43).** Interval labels are decided by deterministic, decision-specific feasibility problems over Θ, never from scenario draws. Primitive domains have a box enclosure (per-row input boxes and a box of declared shared factors, e.g. a common price list or a donor multiple shared by several rows); Θ itself is the constrained subset defined by E73, and a product-box representation may replace it only under a decision-specific equivalence proof (E169). **Constrained evidence model (E73)**: the primitive-variable constraint graph Θ = {v : ℓ ≤ v ≤ u, h(v) = 0, g(v) ≤ 0} is authoritative; costs, quality, capacities and multiples are derived quantities of v (an identity such as API-valued capacity = λ × C_API survives uncertainty propagation). A box reduction is used only with a decision-specific equivalence proof; otherwise the box is an outer enclosure for pruning, and every witness is certified against the original constraints (release fixture: an enclosing box containing a favourable state the original constraints exclude must never return a certified possible membership). **Separability condition (E89)**: with no shared factor **and no row-local coupling** — each row's C and Q depend on disjoint primitives with no joint constraint, so the row's feasible set is the product of its C and Q ranges — each label is decided exactly by monotone extremes (Q is linear in leaf values; basket cost is monotone in component costs; each row's inputs move only its own C and Q): e.g. possible-frontier membership of i is established by setting i to its best corner and every j to its worst. A row-local primitive or constraint that enters both C_i and Q_i is a coupled variable and is branched over like a shared factor; a constraint class the solver does not support returns unresolved (release test: i with C_i = 1 + t, Q_i = t, t ∈ [0, 1]; j with C = 1, Q = 0.5; k with C = 1.5, Q = 1 — i is dominated for every t although its box corner (1, 1) is undominated, so the solver must return disproved and never a possible membership). In v1 every row's C and Q come from distinct published records, so the condition holds and is asserted per release. With shared factors or row-local coupling, labels are decided by **certified interval branch-and-bound** over the shared-factor box: on each sub-box, interval bounds either certify the condition for every point (witness), refute it for every point (prune), or the sub-box is split, up to a published depth; a sub-box still undecided at that depth returns **unresolved**. Endpoint search alone is not sufficient (release test: competitors with Q_j = 2t and Q_k = 2(1 − t), C_j = C_k = 0, and i with C_i = 1, Q_i = 1.2: i is a possible-frontier member via the interior witness t = 0.5). Possible-frontier membership solves, in one common θ, for every j ≠ i: C_j(θ) > C_i(θ) ∨ Q_j(θ) < Q_i(θ) ∨ [C_j(θ) = C_i(θ) ∧ Q_j(θ) = Q_i(θ)]; definite membership is disproved by one θ and one dominating j; strict inequalities are decided exactly, never by tolerance alone. Every returned witness is re-checked against the original equations and shared constraints. The monotone-corner shortcut is used only for decisions with a documented corner-reduction proof; dependencies are compiled from the complete decision equations, so a varying reference quality Q_r is a shared variable for every qualification condition even when all row inputs are independent (E50; a frozen bar level is a constant and adds no shared variable, but the rule binds any registered varying reference): bar decisions partition at qualification breakpoints (exact enumeration for affine one-dimensional thresholds) or use the certified branch-and-bound (regression test: i with C = 1, Q = 5; j with C = 0, Q = 4; Q_r ∈ [0, 10] — i ∈ K_r via the interior witness Q_r = 4.5, which neither endpoint shows). Feasible witnesses and infeasibility certificates have separate proof obligations, and numerical bounds may not discard feasible points through floating-point error. Statuses: certified / disproved / unresolved / unevaluable, with solver status, depth and witness (or counterexample) stored; an unresolved solve is never shown as certified. **Certification grammar (E108)**: each decision is compiled into (1) primitive domains and the original constraints, (2) a reduced problem obtained only by documented, equivalence-preserving substitutions (definitional equalities are eliminated by substitution wherever possible) and (3) a certificate checked independently against the original problem. Supported v1 classes: boxes, affine constraints decided in exact rational arithmetic on the published decimal inputs, separable monotone transforms and the documented corner reductions; a nonlinear equality is supported only with a validated existence certificate (e.g. a sign change of a continuous univariate function evaluated exactly at rational endpoints); every other class returns unresolved, and a small numerical residual is never a feasibility certificate (fixtures: x² = 2 with x ∈ [1, 2] is certified feasible without a rational witness; x² = 2 with x ∈ [1, 1.4] is certified infeasible; an approximate point with residual 10⁻⁶ certifies nothing). **Feasibility prerequisite (E79)**: before any decision, the decision's full constraint component (all connected shared variables and identities) must have a certified feasible witness checked against the original constraints; certified infeasible → "inconsistent evidence", no frontier or bar labels for the affected output, release-blocking for that output with the conflicting records listed; feasibility unresolved → "unresolved", no confirmed or definite label (fixtures: individually valid intervals whose shared identity has an empty intersection; a feasible outer box whose original constrained set is empty — neither may produce a confirmed, definite or certified-possible result).
