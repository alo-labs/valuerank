## Objective and claim scope

AA's Pareto frontier of Intelligence Index vs. total USD cost to run its Intelligence Index does not answer the question ValueRank exists for: **which frontier AI model is actually lowest cost for the value it delivers in practice**, once (a) subscription-plan economics are considered and (b) benchmarks geared to real software-engineering work are weighed alongside general capability. ValueRank serves two buyers, ranked separately: **API buyers** (pay per token, incl. third-party agents) and **Plan buyers** (provider subscription apps/CLIs: Claude Code, Codex, ChatGPT, SuperGrok…).

Claim scope: "value among the configurations ranked in one ranking (configurations AA plots on, or within the near gap of, its rendered frontier that have that ranking's full benchmark set; see Cohort), under that ranking's published benchmark mix and evidence". ValueRank publishes two rankings (owner decision O53): the **max-effort ranking**, which leads (site and README default, O54), and the **AI Coding Daily ranking**. Each has its own leaf set and weights (O55), anchors, bars, scenario run, basket admission and diagnostics (E253, E254); values, ranks and claims compare within one ranking only, never across the two. No configuration is ranked for its evidence alone outside the frontier and near-frontier cohort (O51). Every ranked row is a **configuration** (model version, effort, fallback, route), with a compact configuration ID and an expandable per-leaf execution manifest; tables and claims say "configuration", not "model" (E78). Not a market-wide optimum across all configurations and workloads.

## Source contract (versioned registry)

**Evidence bundle (E159)**: each release is (method version, registry, evidence bundle, extractor versions, outputs). The bundle lists every captured artifact the build reads — source payloads or permitted captures, committed with the release — with its content hash, capture time and extractor, plus a bundle hash; every scored observation records its artifact, field locator and published value. Machine-readable registry per release: AA chart identity, filters and snapshot timestamp (current: "Intelligence Index vs. Cost to Run Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index", x-axis "Cost to Run Intelligence Index (USD)", unit "USD to run all Intelligence Index evaluations", captured 2026-10-23T05:27:39Z for the release cutoff O47 set, previously 2026-10-17); **frontier membership extraction (E195)**: membership source = the rendered Pareto SVG path's vertices matched to the plotted circle centres (tolerance 0.5 px; on 2026-10-17 and again on 2026-10-23 all 18 vertices matched at 0 px), the capture procedure (public chart and tooltips only) and its version; the build fails unless every captured frontier point sits on a path vertex, one point per vertex, and the path rises strictly in chart coordinates (cost and Index both increasing). ## Normative definitions (executable specification)

- **Coverage and scores.** Every definition below is evaluated within one ranking, on that ranking's leaf table, anchors, bars and cohort rows (E253). Leaf weights w_b are percent points summing to 100, and z and u are on 0–100. p_i = Σ_{b∈O_i} w_b / 100. Displayed observed-only quality Q̄_i = Σ_{b∈O_i} w_b z_ib / (100·p_i). Full-composite score Q_i(θ) = (Σ_{b∈O_i} w_b z_ib(θ) + Σ_{b∉O_i} w_b u_ib) / 100, u_ib ∈ [0,100] (fixture: leaves worth 30 points missing, every other leaf at 60 → p_i = 0.70, Q̄_i = 60, Q_i ∈ [42, 72]). **Formal bounds** (E35): Q_i^L = min_{θ∈Θ} Q_i(θ) and Q_i^U = max_{θ∈Θ} Q_i(θ) over the joint admissible set Θ below (measurement intervals of observed leaves and u_ib ∈ [0,100] for missing leaves); "missing at 0/100 at the point estimate" is not the bound. S0 uses Q̄_i; S1/S2 use the full-composite equation. Any sampling law for wholly missing leaves is a declared scenario convention (with one published alternative), never evidence; formal bounds are distribution-free.
- **Joint admissible set Θ (for interval outputs: frontier and bar).** Fixed: nominal weights, primary price rule, frozen route, linear GDPval map (E1). **Target (owner decision O25)**: interval outputs protect the **published record** — the benchmark results and costs as published for each exact configuration (for cost: the published deployment-equivalent cost of the fixed workload). They are robust to the record's reporting precision and to missing evidence, not to run-to-run noise. Benchmark repeatability and fitted-parameter uncertainty (published confidence intervals, run-to-run ranges) are outside the S0–S2 shares and are reported only by separately named streams whose documented estimand supports them (E145, E155; in v1, S1M as a conventional range sensitivity inside Terminal-Bench's published interval, dormant today, E162, E165, and S1T for task sampling, O39); token-use and price-basis variation enter only named S2 specification branches. **Uncertainty descriptions (E155)** are generated from the stream manifests and printed beside every uncertainty output (v1: S0 "published-record reporting-precision scenarios"; S1 "S0 plus conventional missing-evidence scenarios"; S2 core "S1 plus hierarchical weight stress"; S1M "S1 plus conventional range sensitivity inside Terminal-Bench's published 95% confidence interval" (or "dormant: equals S1", E236); S1T "published-record leaf values redrawn with task-sampling noise (independent-task approximation)"; other benchmark repeatability and fitted-parameter uncertainty "not quantified in v1 scenario shares"); a release-text lint rejects any statement that a source interval or run range enters scenario shares unless an active stream's allowlist admits that target. Variable in Θ: rounding intervals of published values at their published precision, missing-leaf and missing-component domains (identification), identified M_W ranges and other structural bounds (e.g. deployment-policy bias bounds). **Constraint basis (O19 under O25)**: each uncertainty object records constraint_basis (rounding of the record / published interval / public repeated runs / structural bound / analyst assumption / score domain), estimand, randomness_unit, constraint_target, coverage_claim and supporting_public_evidence (E46), and separates observed_statistic from target_parameter. **Target-compatibility gate (E49)**: an interval narrows a Θ variable only when its documented constraint_target is that published-record variable (or a public, justified mapping connects them); published confidence intervals and run ranges target other estimands and therefore never enter Θ; a full score domain expresses the absence of a narrower admissible constraint on a missing value. Analyst-assumed allowances never enter Θ. **Completeness (E11)**: every input, whatever its weight, enters Θ through a documented constraint or its full domain; an input with neither makes the affected output "unevaluable" for that row. Missing repeatability evidence never becomes zero uncertainty. Published benchmark intervals are consumed with their stated interpretation. Shared constraints (prices, donor measurements, task/run observations, quota pools) move jointly. With transfers off, a Plan route that depends entirely on an owner-authorised transfer is "unevaluable" in interval outputs (never silently replaced by API cost). Tests: widening Θ cannot move a row from "possible" to "definite" or create a confirmed result; evidence loss is tested in two stages (E38) — the result within R_W may change when a competitor leaves R_W, but the selected-cohort extension can never strengthen.

- **Interval decision procedure (E40, amended by E43).** Interval labels are decided by deterministic, decision-specific feasibility problems over Θ, never from scenario draws. Primitive domains have a box enclosure (per-row input boxes and a box of declared shared factors, e.g. a common price list or a donor multiple shared by several rows); Θ itself is the constrained subset defined by E73, and a product-box representation may replace it only under a decision-specific equivalence proof (E169). **Constrained evidence model (E73)**: the primitive-variable constraint graph Θ = {v : ℓ ≤ v ≤ u, h(v) = 0, g(v) ≤ 0} is authoritative; costs, quality, capacities and multiples are derived quantities of v (an identity such as API-valued capacity = λ × C_API survives uncertainty propagation). A box reduction is used only with a decision-specific equivalence proof; otherwise the box is an outer enclosure for pruning, and every witness is certified against the original constraints (release fixture: an enclosing box containing a favourable state the original constraints exclude must never return a certified possible membership). **Separability condition (E89)**: with no shared factor **and no row-local coupling** — each row's C and Q depend on disjoint primitives with no joint constraint, so the row's feasible set is the product of its C and Q ranges — each label is decided exactly by monotone extremes (Q is linear in leaf values; basket cost is monotone in component costs; each row's inputs move only its own C and Q): e.g. possible-frontier membership of i is established by setting i to its best corner and every j to its worst. A row-local primitive or constraint that enters both C_i and Q_i is a coupled variable and is branched over like a shared factor; a constraint class the solver does not support returns unresolved (release test: i with C_i = 1 + t, Q_i = t, t ∈ [0, 1]; j with C = 1, Q = 0.5; k with C = 1.5, Q = 1 — i is dominated for every t although its box corner (1, 1) is undominated, so the solver must return disproved and never a possible membership). In v1 every row's C and Q come from distinct published records, so the condition holds and is asserted per release. With shared factors or row-local coupling, labels are decided by **certified interval branch-and-bound** over the shared-factor box: on each sub-box, interval bounds either certify the condition for every point (witness), refute it for every point (prune), or the sub-box is split, up to a published depth; a sub-box still undecided at that depth returns **unresolved**. Endpoint search alone is not sufficient (release test: competitors with Q_j = 2t and Q_k = 2(1 − t), C_j = C_k = 0, and i with C_i = 1, Q_i = 1.2: i is a possible-frontier member via the interior witness t = 0.5). Possible-frontier membership solves, in one common θ, for every j ≠ i: C_j(θ) > C_i(θ) ∨ Q_j(θ) < Q_i(θ) ∨ [C_j(θ) = C_i(θ) ∧ Q_j(θ) = Q_i(θ)]; definite membership is disproved by one θ and one dominating j; strict inequalities are decided exactly, never by tolerance alone. Every returned witness is re-checked against the original equations and shared constraints. The monotone-corner shortcut is used only for decisions with a documented corner-reduction proof; dependencies are compiled from the complete decision equations, so a varying reference quality Q_r is a shared variable for every qualification condition even when all row inputs are independent (E50; a frozen bar level is a constant and adds no shared variable, but the rule binds any registered varying reference): bar decisions partition at qualification breakpoints (exact enumeration for affine one-dimensional thresholds) or use the certified branch-and-bound (regression test: i with C = 1, Q = 5; j with C = 0, Q = 4; Q_r ∈ [0, 10] — i ∈ K_r via the interior witness Q_r = 4.5, which neither endpoint shows). ## Known limitations

Full-utilisation Plan cost overstates value for light users (workday diagnostic shown); the Value Multiple values only the API-equivalent consumption of the measured workload, so plan features outside it (chat, image generation, other surfaces, pools shared with them) are not valued; AA cost uses AA's token mix and cache protocol; Terminal-Bench work is priced in two executions; Bug Hunt covers two repositories; Bug Hunt, AI Coding Daily and a tbench.ai gap fill measure model+harness stacks, and AI Coding Daily's harness differs by model; suite is primarily English text; ITBench-AA remains pending until complete exact-variant cost evidence covers two-thirds of a ranking's cohort rows jointly with the admitted components (E95, E101), and the Coding Agent Index v2.0 is pending in the AI Coding Daily ranking (2 of 15, E254), so that ranking's cost basket holds no agentic-coding component and its coding share is far lower than the max-effort ranking's. Independent software-engineering benchmarks run few efforts per model (LiveBench 2.0 and Vals once per model at its top effort, AI Coding Daily mostly at high and medium), so neither ranking covers the cohort: the max-effort ranking ranks 9 of the 29 cohort configurations, the AI Coding Daily ranking 15, and 9 are in neither. The two rankings use different leaves, weights, anchors and bars, so their values, ranks, bands and bar results are not comparable, and a configuration ranked in both has two values. Vibe Code Bench v1.1 is saturated near 90% at the top and LiveBench 2.0 Coding near 96%, so they separate the max-effort rows little. MiMo-V2.6-Pro's added leaves rest on the owner's statement that its single reasoning mode is AA's high configuration (O54, E252). Instruction following has no weight while Surge's Complex Constraints is excluded (O53). On the Plan basis the MiMo multiples are provider-derived quota ceilings, not measurements (O46).
