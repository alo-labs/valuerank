The separability explanation treats positive cross-configuration error correlation as a consequence of using the same tasks. Issue: Part 1, “Outputs → Rank separability,” says that “ρ = 0 widens it, because every row is scored on the same tasks,” and subsequently that shared task difficulty “correlates two rows’ errors and narrows the true paired threshold.” The displayed formula supports narrowing when the relevant covariance is positive; sharing tasks alone does not establish its sign. Configurations with complementary strengths can have negative covariance in task-level performance. Why it hurts the objective: The draft appropriately says the net direction of its approximation is not established. However, the accompanying explanation still presents one component’s direction as established without paired evidence. That can make the noise qualification sound more empirically grounded than it is. Concrete change: Replace the categorical explanation with:

Evaluating configurations on the same tasks creates the possibility of paired dependence. Positive cross-configuration covariance would narrow the threshold relative to ρ = 0; negative covariance would widen it. Its sign and magnitude are not established by the aggregate results captured here.

What is new relative to the ledger: This is not another request to disclose omitted cost noise, add the existing correlation grid, or repeat E340’s net-direction warning. It identifies an unsupported sign assumption inside the explanation of that warning. It changes neither the owner-set scoring policy nor the current diagnostic calculation.
