Critical

High

Cohort boundary is selected on a value definition that mostly contradicts the rankings' own — Owner decision challenge. The near gap (±3 rounded Index points of the frontier's best at equal or lower cost, O129) is computed on AA's Intelligence Index, which in v5 weights Coding at only 25% (Knowledge Work 25%, Science 25%, General 25%), while ValueRank's rankings weight software engineering at 60% (O122). So cohort admission is decided ~75% by non-coding capability, and value/cheapest-above-bar is decided ~60% by coding capability. A configuration that is efficient on FrontierCode/Terminal-Bench/Bug Hunt per dollar but mid-pack on science and general knowledge can sit outside the near gap and be permanently invisible to the objective ("lowest cost for the value it delivers in practice" on an SWE-weighted mix); a general-knowledge-strong configuration with mediocre SWE leaves enters and can win cheapest-above-bar. This is distinct from the rejected "rank every rendered-frontier point / score unranked alternatives" claim: the new argument is a measured definition mismatch between the cohort filter and the ranking's own quality vector, which makes the fixed cohort rule's boundary — not its members — the blind spot. Concrete change (cohort unchanged, implementable from already-captured public data, refreshed routinely): publish per ranking, per release, (a) Spearman ρ between AA Index and ranking quality over the ranked rows, and (b) a cohort-boundary sensitivity list — names only, no scores — of AA-catalog configurations outside the cohort that would fall inside a near gap computed on that ranking's own quality vector over the leaves they have (renormalised, O41-style, no imputation), each labelled "cohort-boundary sensitivity, not a score, not comparable", with their missing leaves. If ρ is high and the list empty, the fixed rule is empirically harmless and the challenge closes; if not, the owner holds the evidence for a versioned migration of the near rule.

Medium

Bar ladder has no ex-ante stability measure at small n. Bars are quartile-ladder positions (floor((n−1)k/4)) over 9 and 20 rows; O145 alone moved 10 configurations through the cohort in one release, and a single row entering or leaving shifts a bar by a whole row's displayed quality. E207 reports drift and currency after the fact, and E199 gives the frontier-families variant, but nothing tells the reader how much a headline bar depends on one or two configurations. Why it hurts: cheapest-above-bar is a headline claim; its fragility is invisible. Concrete change: a leave-one-out sensitivity per ranking and bar — recompute the ladder over the bar population excluding each row in turn, publish each frozen bar's min–max range (or a stable/unstable flag beside the E171-noise-margin companion, O171/E337 machinery already exists to host it).
