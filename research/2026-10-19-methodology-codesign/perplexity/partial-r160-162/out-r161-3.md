1. Findings by severity

Critical

None.

High

O198 published-limit multiples ignore tiered per-token pricing, leaving an unbounded optimistic error on selected Plan rows. Part 6 values OpenCode Go routes as multiple = limit ÷ fee, with DeepSeek at OpenCode's peak prices and Gemini 4 Argon at list prices, and explicitly notes that "a request above a short-context tier counts more per token" — but no rule converts that fact into the multiple. OpenCode Go's limits are per-model monthly dollar amounts drawn down at each model's token price, so a dollar of limit buys fewer tokens on long-context requests; the basket's demand includes long-context agentic work (LiveBench 2.0 Overall includes long agentic tasks), so the eligible API-equivalent consumption is below what limit/fee at base-tier prices implies. The Plan cost (= API cost ÷ multiple) is therefore understated by up to the tier multiplier, in the direction of overstating value. This matters because O198 routes are owner-authorised and selectable (O72): DeepSeek's Plan rows rest on exactly this arithmetic, and unlike MiMo's O46 ceilings — which O125 flags as the optimistic end on use — these routes carry no optimism flag. Concrete change: publish a tier-adjusted bound M ∈ [limit/(fee·τ), limit/fee], where τ is either OpenCode's largest published tier multiplier or the demand-region-weighted multiplier over the basket's long-context share; flag the route "base-tier valuation: optimistic end", and let the route's blocker hull use the bound, mirroring O125/O196. All inputs (OpenCode's tier table, the basket demand region) are public and refreshable.

Medium

The whole controlled-measurement layer of the Plan basis rests on one non-archived paywalled source. E238 pins every Claude and OpenAI controlled measurement to one SemiAnalysis analysis and states the article "is not captured in the evidence bundle, so no content hash is published", while O165 requires archived copies of end-user report pages and O124 requires hashed artifacts for form submissions. If the article's URL goes dark, is silently revised, or access lapses, O130's re-read fails, the release is held (O12), and there is no auditable fallback; a transcription error at selection is also indistinguishable from an article change. This asymmetry — hashed receipts for the least load-bearing evidence, none for the most load-bearing — hurts auditability, which the objective's defensibility depends on. Concrete change: at each O130 re-read, archive the figure-table transcription (and the page itself where licensing permits) with SHA-256 and fetch instant in the evidence bundle, exactly as O165 does for reports; keep selection pinned. This is implementable and refreshable without touching any owner decision.

Comparator key 3 orders unidentified routes by observed multiples measured on incomparable workloads, and that arithmetic sets the official Plan score of workload-unmatched rows (O16). (Owner decision challenge, E93/O48.) An observer who ran a cheap workload reports a smaller observed multiple than an observer of the same plan who ran an expensive one; among unidentified routes the comparator therefore favours routes whose observers happened to run heavy workloads, not routes that are more generous on W. The labels ("observed-workload multiple — target-workload ordering not identified"), the route-alternative table (E171) and the E179 selection summary disclose this well — but the published rank order still encodes the bias. The new argument relative to the ledger's rejection of excluding mix-unmatched routes: the fix below is additive sensitivity, not exclusion, so it cannot reduce coverage. Concrete change: publish a workload-normalised observed multiple as a column in the route-alternative table, M_norm = M_obs × (C_API,config(W_demand) / C_API,config(W_obs)), computed from the configuration's public API prices, the documented measurement workload and the basket demand region; print "not normalizable" where the measurement workload is undocumented. Selection stays untouched; readers can see whether the official ordering survives normalisation.

No row is affected today (no concordant report pairs exist), so the practical harm is nil this release, but the published Plan cost of such a row would be an evidence-priority choice presented as the full-utilisation effective cost. 2. Methodology design recommendations

Prioritised, all implementable from public, routinely refreshable data:

Tier-adjusted bound for O198 routes (closes the High finding). M ∈ [limit/(fee·τ), limit/fee] with τ from OpenCode's published tier table (largest multiplier, or the basket demand region's long-context share applied to the tier schedule); publish the interval beside the point figure, flag "base-tier valuation: optimistic end", and use the bound in the route's blocker hull. Directly analogous to O125's treatment of MiMo ceilings, so it fits the existing grammar.

Archive the SemiAnalysis re-read. At each O130 re-read, store the figure-table transcription (plus page snapshot where licensing permits) with SHA-256 and fetch instant in the evidence bundle, O165-style. Closes the single-source audit gap for the routes that carry the entire Claude/OpenAI Plan layer.

Workload-normalised observed-multiple sensitivity column in the route-alternative table (formula in the Medium finding above), with "not normalizable" where the measurement workload is undocumented. Selection unchanged; makes the E93 label quantitative rather than merely cautionary.

Plan-evidence health line per release: share of Plan-ranked rows whose selected route is (a) measured and unflagged, (b) measured and flagged, (c) assumption or published-limit, (d) API fallback — published as a trend in scores.json. Makes drift toward assumptions visible across releases instead of reconstructible only from the registry.

E179 dominance-delta field naming any lower-grade admissible route with L_a > U_selected, so evidence-priority selections of certifiably dearer routes are visible at row level.

Registry key rename singleProvider → narrowSearch, and the copy-edit of the O196 sentence (both cosmetic, both cheap, both reduce misreading risk in a document whose precision is its main asset).
