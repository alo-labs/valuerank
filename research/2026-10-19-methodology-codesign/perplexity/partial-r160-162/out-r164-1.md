High

The E272/E305 clipped-tolerance formula prints the no-op direction in both branches (part 1, "Transfer tolerance"). The rule states: "a row at cost C above the high anchor c_H needs its multiple multiplied by (C/c_H)·2^(t/d), and one below the low anchor c_L needs it divided by (c_L/C)·2^(t/d)." The magnitudes are correct but the verbs are inverted. Worked check with c_L = $1, c_H = $100, β = 0.25 (so d ≈ 3.76 value points per doubling) and t = 15: a row at C = $400 (above c_H, cost score clipped to 0) must have its multiple divided by (C/c_H)·2^(t/d) ≈ 63 — cost falls to ≈ $6.31, cost score rises to 60, value moves by exactly 15 points. Multiplying its multiple by 63 raises cost to ≈ $25,200 and changes nothing. Symmetrically, a row at C = $0.25 (below c_L, score clipped to 100) must have its multiple multiplied by (c_L/C)·2^(t/d) ≈ 63 (cost ≈ $15.85, score 40, value −15); dividing it moves cost further down and changes nothing. As written, both branches attach the correct factor to the direction that is infinitely robust — the opposite of the sentence's own claim that "the other direction moving nothing." This hurts the objective because the tolerance is the published per-release answer to "how wrong can this transferred Plan multiple be," and a reader or implementer taking the text literally tests robustness in the direction where no value movement is possible. Concrete change: swap the two verbs (above c_H: divided by (C/c_H)·2^(t/d); below c_L: multiplied by (c_L/C)·2^(t/d)), or restate both branches as "the assumed multiple can be wrong by factor X (too high / too low) before the row's value moves by t", and have scores.json clippedTolerance carry an explicit direction field.

Owner decision challenge — O198 admits a provider-published limit as the Plan numerator with no stated rationale, in tension with the fixed Value Multiple rule. The fixed policy bars provider marketing quotas as the Value Multiple numerator; O46's MiMo ceilings are correspondingly labelled "provider-derived, not a measurement" and flagged as the optimistic end on use (O125). O198 instead treats OpenCode's published per-model dollar limits as "its actual limit (multiple = limit / fee, no staged search)." OpenCode Go is real and does publish per-model monthly dollar limits ($10/month base, limits such as $15–$60 per model on Go), but these are provider-marketed figures that have changed and been described by users as confusing, and they are bounded by sub-caps (20% of the limit per 5 hours, 50% per week) that a spread-usage assumption silently ignores. The parts under review never state why a published dollar cap counts as an actual limit while MiMo's credit ceilings count only as a provider-derived ceiling. This hurts the objective because the Plan side's credibility rests on the no-marketing-numerator rule, and one provider class quietly exempted from it is exactly the kind of asymmetry a critic will find. Concrete change, implementable from public data: either (a) state the distinguishing rationale in the Value Multiple evidence section, or (b) align O198 rows with the O46/O125 treatment — flagged as the optimistic end on use, replaced by end-user evidence under the O11/O71 admission rule via the staged search (O73 already allows reports to replace assumption routes; say so for O198 cells), with a labelled sensitivity at half the published limit.
