## 1. Findings by severity

**Critical**

None.

**High**

- **Withdrawn-source reproducibility gap on the largest quality leaf.** Parts 8–9 record that LiveBench withdrew its 2026-10-23 release ("its files now return 404") and that the max-effort ranking pins the 2026-10-25 release under O139. LiveBench 2.0 Overall is 15% of quality, tied with FrontierCode 2.0 as the largest single leaf, and the pinned artifact is no longer publicly fetchable. The O165/O168 SHA-256 archival machinery in part 6 applies only to plan reports, not to board captures. Because third parties can no longer verify 15% of the max-effort quality composite, the objective of a public, routinely refreshable ranking is degraded exactly where weight is highest — and board churn of this kind is structural, not exceptional: LiveBench is frequently re-issued and mirrors routinely lag or go stale, and LiveBench 2.0's Overall is a site-weighted composite the publisher controls. Recommended change: extend the O165 archived-copy-with-hash rule to every pinned third-party artifact (quality boards, the AA chart snapshot, price and plan pages), and add a registry-typed "source release withdrawn" change record with a pre-registered response (keep pinned capture / re-pin / workload re-decision) so the next withdrawal is a routine event rather than an ad-hoc owner call. [benchlm](https://benchlm.ai/benchmarks/livebench)

- **Univariate transfer brackets understate correlated rank risk.** Part 8's O144 route brackets rescore one transferred row at M × min(1, r) and M × max(1, r) "with every other row at its displayed value." But every Claude plan row rests on one donor measurement (Opus 5.5's SemiAnalysis figures) and every non-Sol/non-Astra GPT row on one of two; error in a donor figure or in a shared price ratio r is perfectly correlated across all follower rows. E275 already makes Plan intervals unresolved when rows share an uncertain donor multiple, yet the published `planRouteBrackets` sensitivity — the thing a reader uses to judge how fragile the Plan ordering is — moves rows one at a time, so it cannot show Claude-vs-Claude or GPT-vs-GPT rank reversals that a joint move would produce. Recommended change: publish a joint bracket per donor group (all rows sharing one SemiAnalysis measurement or one lower-of pool moved simultaneously to r_low / r_high), reporting each row's worst-case rank interval and the count of reversals versus the univariate brackets.

**Medium**

- **Owner decision challenge (O84), new argument:** O84 splits every Kimi plan when any Kimi plan changes. This is either redundant with O113 — a change record naming a meter family "reaches every plan on that meter" — or, if Kimi plans sit on distinct meters, over-broad: it discards concordant in-window evidence for unaffected plans and delays their admission by up to a monthly research cycle. Neither tension is resolved in the record of O113 (2026-10-29) versus O84 (2026-10-26). Recommended change: replace the blanket split with a meter-family split consistent with O113, or record once that all Kimi plans share one meter family, which would make O84 a restatement rather than a separate rule.

- **No published sensitivity for two-observer geometric-mean cell values.** Part 7's passing-cell value is the geometric mean of band-clipped observer midpoints ([largest lower ÷ 1.20, 1.20 × smallest upper]). Geometric means are the standard central-tendency choice for ratio-scale quantities, but a passing cell today can have exactly two observers, and a single midpoint sitting at a band edge can move the pooled value by up to a factor of about 1.20^(1/2) ≈ 9.5%. `bandMoved` shows which observers the band clipped, but nothing shows how much each observer contributes to the value. Recommended change: publish a leave-one-observer-out column per passing cell (computable directly from each report's `readings` list), so a value resting on one dominant observer is visible. [cdc](https://www.cdc.gov/environmental-exposure-report/php/about-the-data/geometric-means.html)

**Low**

- **Clip-gate threshold discontinuity.** Part 9 holds a release when "more than max(1, 10% of n) rows of G clip." For n ≤ 10 no single clip ever holds (effective threshold 20%+); at n = 11 two clips hold (~18%). The effective percentage jumps as the gate population crosses n = 10, and the Plan gate population G shrinks by design (measured rows only, O157). Recommended change: publish the effective threshold percentage beside the gate output, or adopt max(1, floor(0.10 n)) so the rule is monotone in n.

- **52/12 weekly conversion feeds scored multiples, not only display.** Parts 6–7 convert a weekly allowance at 52/12 and call it "an annualised display convention that does not establish capacity over any one billing period (E39)" — yet the converted figure is the scored M on weekly-meter routes (Claude plans). A billing month covers 4.28–4.43 weekly windows depending on reset phase, and partial first/last weeks can bind. The error is bounded (a few percent) and the direction is disclosed, so this is low; recommended change: for weekly-meter routes, print the 4-week multiple beside the 52/12 multiple as a named sensitivity.

## 2. Methodology design recommendations

1. **Universal hashed artifact capture (highest priority).** Uniform rule: every artifact the build reads — board captures, the AA rendered-chart snapshot, price pages, plan pages, report mirrors — enters the evidence bundle as (URL or mirror, fetch instant, SHA-256, quoted span), the same shape O165 adopted for plan reports. The build fails when a pinned artifact's hash differs between builds of the same anchor version. Add a typed change record `sourceReleaseWithdrawn`; its default handling (keep pinned capture, disclose; re-pin at next release) is pre-registered so LiveBench-style withdrawals stop requiring emergency owner decisions.
2. **Joint donor-bracket sensitivity.** For each donor group \(G\) (rows whose routes trace to one SemiAnalysis figure or one lower-of pool), rescore \(V^{(G)}_{low}, V^{(G)}_{high}\) with every member moved to its bracket end simultaneously; publish per-row worst-case rank interval \([r^{worst}(G), r^{best}(G)]\) and a reversal count. This complements, not duplicates, E275's interval-output treatment and O25's record-protecting boxes.
3. **Leave-one-observer-out cell statistics.** From `plan_reports.json`, per passing cell compute the cell value with each observer removed; publish \(\Delta\)% per observer next to `bandMoved`. No new evidence needed — pure recomputation over the existing `readings` lists.
4. **Gate-threshold transparency.** Publish `n`, the raw and effective clip thresholds, and the factor-2 count per gate in `scores.json gates.clip`, making the Low finding above a displayed quantity rather than a formula readers must reconstruct.
5. **Detection-lag diagnostic.** For each typed change record, publish the lag between its effective date and its detection date (e.g., the Claude shared-meter change running from 2026-10-17 was detected from user reports and flagged routes later that month). A rolling median lag per provider calibrates how stale "possibly superseded" flags are likely to be — cheap to compute and directly relevant to how much trust flagged-but-still-selected routes deserve.
