## 1. Findings by severity

**Critical**

- **The Plan basis delivers no answer to the Plan buyer the objective names.** The objective explicitly includes "subscription-plan economics" as half of ValueRank's reason to exist, yet the draft states that "no Plan frontier or bar claim is supported", 0 of 30 and 0 of 74 plan rows have an empty flag list, 19 of 30 and 52 of 74 rest on owner assumptions (transfers, quota ceilings, dollar limits), and "no Plan route has an identified M_W". This hurts the objective directly: one of the two buyers gets displayed arithmetic on owner assumptions labelled exploratory, not a ranking that answers "lowest cost for the value delivered". Concrete change, two parts: (a) **Owner decision challenge** on O150 ("O46 stays MiMo-only"): extend the O46/O125 treatment — provider-published ceilings and limits enter as labelled optimistic-end **bands** (a lower bound on Plan cost, upper bound on the multiple), never as the Value Multiple numerator and never label-eligible — to every plan cell where a provider publishes an actual ceiling or dollar limit (OpenCode-style limits under O198 are already precedent for "published limit = actual limit"). This stays inside the fixed policy (no marketing quota as numerator) while converting unsupported point rows into honest bounds. (b) Publish an impact-ordered Plan evidence roadmap (see §2.1) so O164's monthly research targets the cells that block the most labels. What is new versus the ledger: the draft itself now quantifies the emptiness (0/30, 0/74, and the by-kind owner-assumption counts), evidence that did not exist when O72/O150 were adopted; and the banded use of ceilings is categorically different from the rejected numerator use. [livebench](https://livebench.ai/)

**High**

- **The leading ranking has almost no discriminative power.** The max-effort ranking, which "leads on the site and in the README" (O54), has 9 scored rows with 2 of 7 adjacent ranks separable, and the AI Coding Daily ranking has 0 of 19. This hurts the objective because the headline answer to "which model is lowest cost for the value" is, by the draft's own diagnostics, an ordering whose neighbouring rows are mostly statistical ties. Concrete change: make the O42 noise bands the primary display (ranks secondary), and print each ranking's separable-pair share beside the common-leaf ranking's (O121, 33 rows), which may separate more. **Owner decision challenge** on O54: the separability counts were not known when the lead ranking was chosen; if a future release shows the common-leaf ranking separating materially more pairs, the lead should follow discriminative power. - **Rule-surface complexity has outrun the evidence base.** Parts 1–2 alone carry dozens of single-configuration exceptions (O45 per-plan GPT figures, O46 MiMo ceilings, O66 Kimi at max, O84 Kimi plan splits, O135/O140/O148/O149 MiMo and MiniMax effort matching, O153 Cognition fills) layered on a 9-row headline and a Plan table with zero supported rows. Concrete change: convert configuration-specific exceptions into a typed, parameterised mechanism (one flag class with per-row parameters, the way O100/O103 classify AA notes by exact text), and publish a per-release manifest of which rules were touched, so a routine refresh does not require re-reading the ledger. **Medium**

- **Bug Hunt's live board-max rescaling breaks cross-release comparability by design.** O170 rescales z by the board maxima "live each release", so an unchanged configuration's quality — and hence its value score and rank movement — can change between releases purely because the board's maxima moved. This hurts the objective because a buyer comparing releases cannot tell model change from scale change. Concrete change: publish a scale bridge each release (each previously-ranked configuration's Bug Hunt z recomputed under the current denominator) and a README statement that value scores are within-release measures; also disclose that the denominator (board-max planted + board-max unplanted) is a synthetic composite no configuration need attain. A buyer can see the bar answer flip with no model or price change. Concrete change: a cross-release bar-answer stability diagnostic — recompute the current release's cheapest-above-bar answer under the previous release's frozen bar levels and list the flips with their attributed cause (cohort churn vs quality change vs re-freeze). ## 2. Methodology design recommendations

1. **Plan evidence roadmap (highest priority).** Extend E278's model×plan enumeration into a published priority queue: for each cell, priority = (number of interval labels currently blocked by that cell) × (plan rows affected), with the staged-search stage (O71/O75) that will next touch it and the hashing requirement of O124 attached. Success metric: labels unblocked per release. This converts the O164 monthly research budget from coverage-driven to decision-driven.
2. **Banded ceilings for all providers (Owner decision challenge on O150).** For each plan cell with a provider-published ceiling or dollar limit, publish the plan-cost band [fee, fee × (limit/ceiling-quota)] with the O46-style "provider-derived, optimistic end" flag, scored only in scenario outputs and never label-eligible — the O46/O125 mechanism, generalised.
3. **Bands-first display.** Keep ordinal ranks (O2/O22) but order the public table by the E328 noise bands; print each ranking's separable-pair share and the common-leaf ranking's beside them, so the lead choice (O54) has a measured discriminative basis.
4. **Cost-noise separability grid.** Recompute the E277 within-workload adjacent-pair thresholds with cost-score relative SD ∈ {0%, 5%, 10%} added in quadrature; publish the separable count at each grid point with the "assumption grid, not evidence" label, mirroring the existing ρ grid.
5. **Bug Hunt scale bridge and synthetic-ceiling disclosure** (as in §1 Medium).
6. **Cross-release bar-answer stability diagnostic** (as in §1 Medium).
7. **Exception governance.** One typed flag schema for configuration-specific rules, with parameters in the registry rather than new O/E numbers; a per-release manifest of rules touched.
8. **Missing-telemetry cost bracket (conditional on Q1).** If O27's scenario layer does not already do this: recompute the Coding Agent Index cost with missing-telemetry tasks priced at the configuration's mean per-task cost and at the cohort maximum, bracketing the direction of the bias. AA itself notes it uses provider token counts "where available", so non-random missingness (e.g. failed or timed-out runs) is plausible.
