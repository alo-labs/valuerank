## 1. Findings by severity

**Critical**

None.

**High**

1. **Metering-type change records can never flag a pre-dating measurement, but debit-rate metering changes alter attainable consumption exactly like a limit change.** Part 6 types OpenAI's Day 1/Day 2 posts and its later caching and context-reuse posts as "metering," and O101 states an announcement that "changes only how usage is metered is logged and flags nothing, as it splits nothing under O99"; O107's flagging list is limit, price and credit only . The non-splitting rationale is sound, but flagging is separate machinery: a change to how many meter units a token of a category the basket uses debits (cache rates, context-reuse discounts, tier multipliers) changes the maximum API-equivalent consumption a fixed meter can deliver — the very quantity the E210 bound reasons about — so an older controlled measurement becomes silently stale with no supersession signal and no O78 re-ranking trigger. It hurts the objective because the official Plan ranking's multiples can go wrong between releases with no reader-visible flag; today's release is protected only incidentally, because the 2026-10-11 price change already flags every GPT route. *Owner decision challenge (O101/O77/O83): the stated rationale covers partition-splitting, not measurement flagging.* Concrete change: split the metering type into "consumption-relevant metering" (any change to the debit per token for a category the basket's demand region uses) which flags measurements predating it without splitting partitions, and "display/counting metering," which stays non-flagging; the freshness check already finds and logs these announcements, so no new data source is needed.

**Medium**

2. **The OpenCode Go multiple (O198) is a point chosen inconsistently with the draft's own full-utilisation definition, and one-sidedly optimistic for long requests.** The multiple is taken at OpenCode's peak prices (off-peak use "would double it") while the full-utilisation definition is the "maximum eligible API-equivalent consumption attainable"  — maximum attainment would use the off-peak debit where the window allows, so the peak choice understates by up to 2×; conversely the multiple is taken at the short-context tier, which overstates by up to 5× for Haiku 5.5 long requests ("their Plan rows are optimistic for long requests by that much"). The two adjustments pull in opposite directions and neither matches the maximum-attainment definition. *Owner decision challenge (O198/O199) in part; the new argument is the internal inconsistency with the full-utilisation definition, which the ledger's O198 rationale does not address.* Concrete change: set-identify the OpenCode multiple as an interval \[limit/fee at the most expensive applicable debit basis, limit/fee at the cheapest\], intersected with the tier multiplier range, scored per the existing interval machinery (E67), or at minimum publish the bracket as a per-row sensitivity and score the conservative end.

3. **E210's fixture rationale contradicts its own formula and the derivation's assumptions are unstated.** The fixture says x = (1, 1), u = (1, 0) "leaves M_W unbounded, since the second category's burn is unconstrained"  — but with u₂ = 0 the basket never burns category 2 (the measurement did, x₂ = 1); what actually happens is that ρ₂ = x₂/u₂ = ∞ makes the enclosure [ρ_min/ρ_max, ρ_max/ρ_min] vacuous, which is looseness, not unboundedness of M_W. The enclosure itself is valid under the natural model (single additive token pool, values at published per-category prices, where M_W/M_obs reduces to the ratio of price-weighted averages of the two mixes, and the ρ-extreme enclosure follows from the weighted-average ratio lemma) — but the draft never states that model, or whether x and u are token counts or shares, so an implementer cannot check it before E210 activates on a published cache split and starts set-identifying M_W for interval outputs. Concrete change: pin the derivation and its assumptions in the text, correct the fixture's stated reason, and consider the tighter computable bound (worst case over the demand region of the price-weighted-average ratio using published per-category prices) instead of the looser ρ-extreme enclosure.

4. **E194's publication-date fallback makes the controlled-measurement clock anti-conservative.** For the SemiAnalysis analysis, "no separate measurement window, so the E194 observation date is the publication date" (2026-10-06) . A controlled measurement can conclude weeks before publication; ageing from publication keeps the evidence Current longer than the underlying run, and — more importantly — a limit/price/credit change dated between the true measurement end and publication would not flag under O107's "dated after it" test even though it postdates the run. Concrete change: require the selected analysis's stated measurement window (transcribed at selection, like the figure table) to set the observation end; where a source states none, flag "observation end not evidenced; publication date used" and treat changes dated within the source's own stated research period as postdating the measurement.

**Low**

5. **"Single-source routes are diagnostics" is ambiguous as written.** The sentence sits in the O11 admission discussion , but every currently admitted controlled-measurement route is single-source (one SemiAnalysis analysis), so a literal reading contradicts the admission rule it sits beside. Concrete change: reword to "report-based routes with fewer than two independent reports are diagnostics."

6. **The published "±20%" concordance label does not match the operational test.** O11's phrasing "concordant within ±20%" is implemented as max/min ≤ 1.20 (100/120 pass; 90/110, both within ±10% of their midpoint, fail) . The stricter test is fine; the label invites misreading. Concrete change: publish the operational form ("largest/smallest ≤ 1.20") wherever "±20%" appears in outputs.

7. **The weekly SemiAnalysis watch is report-only with no build check.** A silent lapse in the weekly scheduled search would delay detection of a revised figure until the O130 30-day re-read gate fires . Concrete change: add a build-checked freshness requirement on the watch log (latest entry ≤ 7 days before V), analogous to the E218 freshness check.

## 2. Methodology design recommendations

Prioritised, all implementable from routinely refreshable public data:

1. **Consumption-relevant metering subtype (pairs with Finding 1).** Registry field on each metering record: `debitRatesChanged: {category → (old, new) meter units per token}` derived from the provider's post. If any changed category intersects the basket's demand region, the record flags (never splits) every controlled measurement and report partition whose observation end predates the announcement date; O78's re-ranking then applies as for limit/price/credit changes. No new evidence source — the freshness check already captures these announcements.

2. **Set-identified OpenCode Go multiple (pairs with Finding 2).** Multiple ∈ \[(limit/fee) × min tier factor at cheapest debit, (limit/fee) × 1 at most expensive debit\], computed from OpenCode's published tier prices and off-peak rates and AA's price list; enter the existing E67 interval machinery rather than a point, and keep the "published dollar limit" capacity status. This removes the one-sided long-request optimism and reconciles the row with the maximum-attainment definition.

3. **Controlled-measurement observation-end transcription (pairs with Finding 3 in Section 1).** At selection and each O130 re-read, transcribe the source's stated measurement period into `sourceEvidence.semianalysis.measurementWindow`; E194 then reads its end. Flag where absent.

4. **Pin and tighten E210 (pairs with Finding 3).** State the additive-pool model, define x and u as token vectors, fix the fixture, and compute the tighter bound as the worst case over the demand region of Σβ_k p_k / Σα_k p_k (α, β = measured and target category token shares, p = published per-category prices), falling back to the ρ-extreme enclosure only where per-category prices are unavailable.

5. **Plan evidence health panel.** One published block per release: Plan rows by evidence grade (controlled measurement / concordant reports / owner assumption / published limit / quota ceiling / API fallback), count and share carrying change flags, and count of routes whose only evidence is the single selected analysis. Today that panel would show 0 report-admitted routes and every measured route flagged — the single-source dependence is fully disclosed in prose but should be visible at headline level.

6. **Watch-log freshness build check** (Finding 7): `sourceEvidence.semianalysis.watch` with a ≤ 7-day gate, failing the build like the other clocks.

7. **Label hygiene** (Findings 5–6): reword "single-source routes are diagnostics" and publish "largest/smallest ≤ 1.20" in place of "±20%."
