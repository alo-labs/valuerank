Medium

O28 residual tolerance is not derivable from AA's displayed surfaces as cited. The gate stops the build on a reconstruction residual above 0.05 index points, justified as "half AA's displayed precision". AA's public surfaces mix granularities: the AA-Omniscience page's headline prose and AA's own v1.1 announcement display the Net Score as integers (67, 65, 64 on the page; 54, 48, 46 in the announcement), while the page's v1 → v1.1 difference panel displays one-decimal values (+8.9, +1.7, −0.8, …), implying one-decimal scores in the per-row tables. Why it hurts: if the check reads an integer-displayed surface, rounding alone produces residuals up to 0.5, so the gate would spuriously hold releases (O12) on most captures — breaking routine refreshability; if it reads a one-decimal surface, the number is right but the justification is unverifiable unless the surface is named, and an AA display change would silently re-mean the gate. Concrete change: name the captured surface in the registry, record its smallest displayed increment per capture, and set the tolerance to half that increment (0.5 for an integer surface, 0.05 for one-decimal), with the build stopping when the granularity itself changes; keep the reviewer-81 propagated half-width add-on.

Provider-claimed rows can set the bar level that governs other rows' labels. E92, as amended by E233, keeps a provider-claimed row in the bar population, so its unverified displayed quality can sit at a ladder position and thereby set H (O34) — the level every confirmed-cheapest and qualification label tests against. Why it hurts: E233 exists so unverified provider evidence cannot shape label outcomes; through the bar channel it can — one provider-claimed row can move the median position by a slot and shift H, denying labels to fully verified rows (or easing them at the next re-freeze). This is the same denial-of-label channel the blocker machinery closes for direct competition, arriving through the threshold instead. What is new versus E233 as adopted: E233 settled the row's own label eligibility, not its role in setting the label-governing level; nothing in the ledger addresses bar-population membership of provider-claimed rows. Concrete change: exclude rows with any provider-claimed leaf (the E233 register already tracks them) from the bar population, taking effect at the next versioned re-freeze with the E197 dual run; until then, publish a barCurrency-style companion ladder computed without provider-claimed rows and flag any bar answer whose H would move under it.

Low

E226's common-leaf diagnostic is all-or-nothing at small n. "At least 90% of the ranking's ranked rows" over the max-effort ranking's 9 rows means a leaf held by 8 of 9 rows (88.9%) is dropped entirely, so the diagnostic's granularity is one whole row and the leaf-availability effect it once measured cannot show up in partial form. Why it hurts: a diagnostic that cannot distinguish "one row missing the leaf" from "all rows missing it" gives the owner no signal in exactly the small rankings where a single admission gap is most likely. Concrete change: pre-register a small-n retention rule (e.g., retain a leaf when the number of rows holding it is at least max(⌈0.9n⌉, n − 1) for n < 12), or publish beside the diagnostic the minimum row count that flips each leaf in or out. Tolerance derivation for the O28 reconstruction check (from Medium 1).
