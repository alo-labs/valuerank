## 1. Findings by severity

**Critical**

None.

**High**

1. **Owner decision challenge — O198's OpenCode Go multiple (`limit / fee`) is not derived under the draft's own Plan estimand.** Part 15 declares the λ / time-indexed LP "the **only governing Plan formula**" and E69 defines the scored Plan target as **token-equivalent (API-priced) capacity**; E168 requires every amount to record its derivation and warns that M × F reproduces an amount only within one measurement object. Yet O198's route is scored by a bare division, `limit / fee`, with "no staged search", and nowhere do these parts state the meter's price basis. A hard cap of $10 on *metered* use yields API-equivalent capacity of (limit ÷ the seller's meter price per token) × API price per token — which equals limit/fee only if OpenCode's meter prices tokens at exactly the API list prices used for C_i,W. Meter-rate divergence from API rates is the norm, not the exception: AA's own published data shows per-task cost varying ~9x across providers for the same model purely from cache pricing and hit rates, and cache/write pricing splits mean a dollar of metered use rarely buys a dollar of C_i,W-valued consumption. It also bypasses the LP's min-over-pools logic, so any time-windowed sub-cap is ignored. Why it hurts: the row is scored in the official Plan ranking with measurement-grade standing, so buyers comparing it to genuinely measured rows assume a common estimand — precisely the confusion E166/E168 exist to prevent. Recommended change: run OpenCode Go through the E69/E168 machinery — record the meter denomination and per-token price basis (O126-style), and compute capacity with the LP treating the dollar cap as one budget B_w; if the meter's price basis is not public, publish the row as a provider-derived ceiling carrying the O125-style "optimistic end" flag rather than as a measurement. O199/O203 (the row stays listed and admitted) are unchanged. 

**Medium**

2. **Scenario draws are reproducible only per CPython version, and a routine environment refresh silently breaks cross-release comparability of scenario shares.** The draft correctly notes that Python guarantees only `random()` across versions, records the drawing Python version, and treats a version change "like a change of draw order"  — consistent with the CPython documentation. But under O25 scenario shares are the *only* channel where run-to-run noise appears, so a Python upgrade during a routine refresh invalidates the one longitudinal noise signal the methodology publishes, and the mitigation is disclosure rather than elimination. Recommended change: implement the Beta and normal transforms in-repo on top of `random()` (the one guaranteed-stable primitive), or use numpy's `Generator` with a fixed, named BitGenerator; add a canary-draw fixture that asserts hash equality of a fixed-seed draw panel across environments; keep recording seed, generator and order. 

3. **Normative rules in these parts exist only as dense prose, which caps public auditability.** The change-detection passage in Governance is a single sentence of several hundred words embedding eight owner decisions (O99, O105, O107, O110, O111, O77, O101, O79) with nested date and precedence logic; the E212 lifecycle table and the release-test list are similarly overloaded. The draft's own E265 philosophy — "the specification never cites a generated document, because no check can verify that a document prints what a sentence promises"  — applies equally to rule prose: no check can verify the prose states what the build implements. For a public ranking whose value rests on being checkable by third parties, this is an auditability gap, not a correctness one. Recommended change: emit a structured rules registry (rule id, trigger, action, fixtures, owning decision) from the same source that renders the draft, and extend the E265 check to fail when a rule cited in prose has no registry entry whose trigger/action summary matches, or when a registry rule appears in no prose section. 

**Low**

4. **Plan lifecycle evidence quality is disclosed only in Known limitations, not at the point of use.** Most Plan rows rest on possibly-superseded routes (21 of 30 and 60 of 74) and some on fault-window measurements (3 and 12); individual rows carry their labels, but the aggregate is buried in a limitations paragraph. Recommended change: publish a value-score-weighted lifecycle-stage share beside the Plan table (e.g. scores.json `plan.lifecycleShare`), refreshed each release, so a reader sees at a glance that the Plan ordering largely rests on flagged evidence. 

5. **The Bug Hunt harness sensitivity does not state what stays frozen.** Part 15 says the sensitivity sets each configuration's leaf to its lowest and highest harness result and publishes the largest value movement and rank changes; it does not say that anchors, bars and other rows' displayed values are held fixed (which O25 and O170's board-based scale imply). One sentence would close the ambiguity about whether the sensitivity moves one row or the whole panel. 

6. **Owner decision challenge — O197's fully automated search conflates evidence discovery with evidence admission.** The staged search cannot reach Reddit directly, X (HTTP 402) or YouTube, and reads LINUX DO only through a mirror — precisely where Kimi, Xiaomi and MiniMax end-user reports concentrate. Today zero rows sit on API fallback, so there is no current ranking harm; but a future "no concordant pair" outcome for those providers would partly reflect where the search could look, and the Plan ranking would then mix estimands. The new argument against O197 as stated: automation could be required for the *admission tests* (O11 independence, O74 concordance, E221 dating) while evidence *discovery* is allowed a logged manual stage whose finds must still pass those tests. At minimum, surface the per-provider search-reach status at Plan-row level rather than only in the registry. 

## 2. Methodology design recommendations

Prioritized:

1. **Unify O198 with E69 (fixes Finding 1).** Add to the O198 route record: `meter_denomination`, `meter_price_basis` (per-token rates and cache treatment, or "not public"), and a `derivation` field per E168. Compute the multiple as min over pools via the LP with the dollar cap as B_w, converted to API-equivalent consumption at the recorded meter rates. If the meter rates are not public, type the row as a provider quota ceiling with the O125 optimistic-end flag. This is implementable today from OpenCode's published plan terms and the existing LP code.

2. **Environment-independent stochastic layer (fixes Finding 2).** Replace `random.betavariate`/`random.gauss` with in-repo transforms of `random()` (e.g. inverse-CDF or Box–Muller implemented once), or numpy `Generator(PCG64(seed))`. Add a CI fixture drawing a 1,000-value canary panel at a fixed seed and comparing its SHA-256 against a committed digest, so any environment that changes draws fails the build instead of silently reordering them. Keep `scenarios.generator` and add `scenarios.sampler_revision`.

3. **Generated rules appendix (fixes Finding 3).** Move each normative rule into a YAML/JSON registry (id, owner decision, trigger, action, fixtures, prose anchor) that both the build and the rendered draft consume; the E265 check then verifies bidirectional coverage. This also gives the E63 active-rules manifest a machine-checked source of truth for gate status instead of a hand-maintained list.

4. **Plan lifecycle share at point of use (fixes Finding 4).** A one-line table companion: share of ranked Plan rows (and of value-score weight) in stages Current / Possibly superseded / Expired / Fault-window, from existing E212 data — no new evidence needed.

5. **Held-release sensitivity for cutoff-day Bug Hunt rows.** Part 17 stops the build when a Bug Hunt record is dated on or after the cutoff's day "because the owner's notes give no instant". Consider adding a published count of how often this gate fired historically, so readers can distinguish a data-freshness hold from a methodology defect when a release is held.
