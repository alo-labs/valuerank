**Medium**

1. **The separability paragraph's cost justification conflates two different costs.** Part 1 states cost is not drawn in the separability threshold because "AA publishes one cost pass per configuration and no per-task cost dispersion for the Index evaluations, so no public cost SD exists." That is true of the Intelligence Index total (AA documents it as one aggregate computed from token prices and tokens used across evaluations ), but the *scored* API cost is the SDLC basket (O15), whose dominant component, the Coding Agent Index, is defined (O27) as a pooled **per-task** cost average — and AA explicitly reports "cost, token usage and execution time per task" for it, marketing it as "average pay-per-token API cost per task". So task-level cost dispersion for at least the priced basket components is public and already inside the evidence bundle. Why it hurts: the published "no public cost SD exists" claim is inaccurate for the cost actually scored, and the E340 disclosure ("omitting cost noise narrows separability") understates what is computable — the separable counts (2 of 7, 0 of 19) rest on a narrower omission than the draft says. Change: reword the justification to distinguish the one-pass Intelligence Index cost (a diagnostic) from the per-task-priced basket, and compute a task-resampling cost SD for the components that publish per-task costs (see R1), published as a companion separable count. [artificialanalysis](https://artificialanalysis.ai/models)

2. **E205's one-row-per-family headline bars cannot be reconciled from the draft.** Part 1 gives the max-effort config ladder as 49.4 / 61.4 / 67.5 over 9 configurations and E205's one-row-per-family headline bar as 67.4 (and 37.5 for AI Coding Daily). The max-effort ranking's 9 configurations come from 9 distinct families, so if all 9 were family primaries the E205 panel would coincide with the ranked set and its lower-median bar would be 61.4, not 67.4 — implying some ranked max-effort rows are non-primary efforts of their family, but the draft never enumerates ranked non-primary points (it only lists the four *excluded* ones), and E205's panel is not among the populations that part 2 says are "published with their member IDs." Why it hurts: E265 holds that every figure is rendered from the build and checked, yet this published figure is not traceable to its panel from the draft; a reader cannot tell 67.4 from a mis-selected ladder rung (the config ladder's upper quartile is 67.5) or from a genuine smaller panel. Change: publish `diagnostics.familyPrimary` member IDs and the count, and state in the Cohort section which ranked rows are non-primary efforts of their family.

3. **The near gap measures quality distance only, with no cost bound (Owner decision challenge).** The cohort rule (fixed policy) admits configurations "within 3 points of the frontier's best at equal or lower cost." For an expensive configuration the reference is the global frontier maximum, so a configuration 10× costlier than the top frontier point and 2 Index points below it is "near-frontier," while one 3.5 points below at 2% higher cost is out. Why it hurts: the objective is lowest cost for value delivered; a cohort labelled Pareto + near-Pareto that admits arbitrarily cost-dominated entrants lets such rows enter bar populations and panels, while the exclusion edge is driven purely by a rounded-Index cutoff. Change (as a challenge, leaving the rule itself to the owner): publish a cost-domination diagnostic — for each near row, the cost ratio to its dominating frontier point, with a flagged count above a threshold (e.g., 2×) — or add a cost-distance bound to the gap.

## 2. Methodology design recommendations

Prioritised, all implementable from public, refreshable data:

1. **Partial task-sampling cost SD (addresses M1).** For each basket component with published per-task costs (Coding Agent Index at minimum — AA reports per-task cost, tokens and time ), compute a bootstrap or jackknife SE over the task set (290 tasks at v2.0 under O27), propagate to the row's total basket cost through the component's expenditure share (E173 already publishes shares), and convert to the value scale via the anchor exchange rate d (value points per cost doubling = 100·β·ln2 / ln(c_H/c_L), β = 0.25): SE_value(cost) = SE(ln C_total) × d. Publish a second separable count beside the current one, labelled "cost-noise-inclusive (priced components only)," flag rows whose cost lacks per-task telemetry, and keep the primary threshold unchanged — this is a disclosure companion in the spirit of E340, not a change to O39. [artificialanalysis](https://artificialanalysis.ai/methodology/coding-agents-benchmarking/)

2. **E205 panel publication (addresses M2 of Medium).** Add member IDs, panel size and the primary-selection reason per family to `diagnostics.familyPrimary`, and enumerate ranked non-primary rows in the Cohort section so the 67.4 / 37.5 figures are checkable against E193's rule.

3. **Near-gap cost-domination diagnostic (supports the M3 challenge).** For every cohort row, publish the cost ratio to its dominating frontier point and a flagged count of rows above a chosen factor. This leaves O129 untouched while making the cost-blindness of the gap visible each release.

4. **Plan flag cross-tab (addresses Low 1).** A rendering change only: a joint count matrix over the flag kinds (superseded, fault-window, valuation-basis-unrecorded, owner-assumption kinds) beside the existing per-kind counts, so overlaps and coincidences like the duplicated 21/60 are self-evident.

5. **Close the sub-frontier edge case (addresses Low 3).** One sentence in the near-gap rule plus a build check.
