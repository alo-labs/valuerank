**Medium**

- **M1 — The S1T Terminal-Bench kernel ignores the trial counts every public executor documents, making it ~2–4× wider than any source-supported reading.** Part 11 sets the Terminal-Bench leaf's S1T kernel at the published task count (n = 66) with the independent-task binomial and states "attempts averaged within a task are ignored." But TB 4.0's task count of 66 is confirmed, and every public run of it repeats trials: AA's own Terminal-Bench 4.0 evaluation reports pass@1 averaged over three repeats per task, and the tbench.ai board runs five trials per task, with the n_trials field already captured in the registry (E162). At a typical cohort score (p ≈ 0.60), the n = 66 kernel gives SD ≈ 6.0 points (implied 95% ±11.8), while tbench.ai prints ±3.1 for its 64.8% leader (SD ≈ 1.6)  and the whiskers it prints are explicitly the 95% confidence interval; trials-aware independent approximations give SD ≈ 3.5 (AA, 198 trials) to 2.7 (tbench, 330 trials). Why it hurts: Terminal-Bench carries 11–15 quality points (O55/O61), so an overstated kernel inflates the O44/O128 noise margin and pushes separability toward zero ("0 of 30 adjacent ranks separable") — the ranking under-claims distinguishable adjacent ranks, a core comparative output, and the error is in the calibration, not the labelling. Concrete change: for the AA-run leaf, publish a trials-aware variant with the binomial denominator scaled by the documented repeats (66 × 3 = 198, labelled "trials-aware independent approximation") beside the primary task-count kernel, and add a per-leaf calibration ratio — the kernel's implied 95% width divided by the source's own printed 95% width — beside every separability statement; today that ratio is ~3.8 for Terminal-Bench and would flag the outlier automatically. Every input needed (n_trials, AA's repeat count, the printed whiskers) is already captured. [llm-stats](https://llm-stats.com/benchmarks/terminal-bench-4.0)

**Low**

- **L1 — Numeric/wording inconsistency in the Jeffreys-vs-plug-in comparison.** Part 11 says the Beta draw's variance is "smaller by a factor √(n/(n+2)) (1.5% at 66 tasks, 0.2% at 1,000)". √(n/(n+2)) is the **SD** ratio, not the variance ratio (which is n/(n+2)); and at n = 1,000 the SD reduction is √(1000/1002) ≈ 0.1%, not 0.2% (0.2% is the variance reduction; the 1.5% figure at n = 66 is the SD reduction). One of the two parenthetical examples is on the wrong scale. The Jeffreys posterior itself, Beta(k + ½, n − k + ½) with mean (k + ½)/(n + 1), is standard and correct. Why it hurts: E265 says every figure is rendered and checked; this one reads as hand-derived and self-contradictory. Concrete change: state "the SD is smaller by √(n/(n+2)) (1.5% at 66 tasks, 0.1% at 1,000)" and let the build emit both percentages. [metricgate](https://metricgate.com/docs/jeffreys-interval/)
- **L2 — The published analytic SD and the actual S1T Beta kernel rest on different variances, and only one number is published.** Part 11 discloses (E353) that the analytic SD is the plug-in value while the O142 draw has variance p̃(1 − p̃)/(n + 2), "so the plug-in summary is the larger of the two" — but only the plug-in is published as "the analytic SD". A reader comparing it to the realised Monte Carlo dispersion of S1T shares sees a mismatch of up to ~3% at small n. Concrete change: publish both SDs per binomial leaf in the per-leaf noise object, and state in `scenarios.streamDescriptions` which kernel each published noise quantity (O42 bands, O128 margin, separability) actually consumes.
- **L3 — E154 promises "an explicitly registered exact algorithm" for uniform volume on a feasible polytope, which does not exist in general.** Exact uniform sampling on an arbitrary polytope is unavailable in bounded time; any real sampler is asymptotically mixing. Dormant today (every v1 component is a singleton), but the normative wording lets a future multi-record component claim exactness the machinery cannot deliver. Concrete change: reword to "a registered sampler with a published mixing/coverage diagnostic within its resource limit, else deterministic bounds," keeping the existing fallback.

## 2. Methodology design recommendations

1. **Trials-aware kernel variant plus a per-leaf calibration-ratio diagnostic (from M1, top priority).** For each leaf whose source documents repeated trials, publish beside the primary task-count kernel a variant with denominator n = tasks × documented repeats (AA Terminal-Bench: 66 × 3 = 198; a tbench.ai gap fill: 66 × n_trials ), and publish `kernelImplied95 / sourcePrinted95` wherever the source prints an interval (tbench whiskers are its 95% CI; today ≈ 3.8). This is a scenario-layer change only — no score, label or Θ output moves — and is implementable from data already in the capture. [artificialanalysis](https://artificialanalysis.ai/evaluations/terminalbench-4-0)
2. **Two-number SD publication and kernel attribution (from L2).** Add `analyticSd.pluginIn` and `analyticSd.betaDraw` per binomial leaf, and one sentence in the stream description naming the kernel behind each published noise quantity, so the analytic summaries and the Monte Carlo frequencies cannot be read as inconsistent.
3. **LiveBench 2.0 Overall kernel decomposition.** The Overall SD is the weighted variance bound over the seven categories' task counts; at the draft's counts, Data Analysis (n = 3) and Reasoning (n = 4) contribute roughly three-quarters of the bound's variance, and a consumer cannot see this from a single SD. Publish each category's contribution share to the Overall kernel beside the separability figures. The category set itself matches the live site's seven suites (Long Agentic, Reasoning, Coding, Browser, Data Analysis, Research, Design), so the counts are re-checkable at each capture. [livebench](https://livebench.ai/)
4. **Apply the L1 and L3 wording fixes** so the published derivations remain machine-checkable under E265 and the dormant polytope clause cannot promise exactness.
