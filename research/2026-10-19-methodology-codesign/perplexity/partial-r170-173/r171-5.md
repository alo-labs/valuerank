## 1. Findings by severity

**Critical**

None. The parts are internally consistent (weights sum to 100 in all three rankings; the 66%/34% and 78%/22% source shares and the 51%/60%/60% coding-specific shares all recompute correctly from the printed leaf table), and the anchor, gate and migration machinery is coherently specified with fixtures.

**High**

1. **The cost term's realized influence is anchor-width-dependent and unbounded, so "cost 25%" does not mean the same thing across bases or releases.** With S_C linear in log cost between c_L and c_H, the value cost of a cost doubling is 25 × 100·ln2 / ln(c_H/c_L). The draft itself prints the consequence: 3.11 value points per doubling (max-effort API), 5.31 (max-effort Plan), 2.71 and 4.11 on the AI Coding Daily bases — a 70% gap between the two bases of the same ranking, driven purely by the reference panel's cost spread, not by buyer economics. Nothing floors the anchor log-span; O157's measured-only Plan anchor panels (minimum two references) make narrow spans more likely, which inflates cost's influence further. E189/E196 disclose the variance allocation, but disclosure does not stabilise the headline metric. Why it hurts: a Plan buyer comparing value scores across bases or releases is comparing scores in which identical nominal weights deliver materially different cost sensitivity. Recommended change: publish λ (value points per doubling) per basis and ranking in every headline output beside the nominal 25%; fix a methodology-version-level minimum anchor log-span (e.g., max(observed span, ln 50)) with the O12 dual-run bridge; add an E177 named swing class ("anchorSpan") when ln(c_H/c_L) moves more than 2× between releases.

**Medium**

2. **The Plan clip-gate population G is not enumerated against the Plan row types the methodology now creates.** The gate text excludes only "the owner-assumption and API-cost-fallback rows O157 sets outside the gate", while O157's own anchor-exclusion list names three types (assumption, fallback, expired measurement), and the ranked Plan population per the ledger includes O46/O125 provider-derived MiMo ceilings, O88 donor-following routes (measured evidence per O90, hence plausibly anchor-eligible even though the figure is another effort's), and O118/O119 in-grace/fault-window rows. Why it hurts: a misclassification either holds releases (O12) on a provider-derived ceiling that is explicitly "not a measurement", or lets far-off-anchor rows escape the gate; and an implementer cannot derive n = |G| from this section alone. Recommended change: publish a row-type × gate-membership × anchor-eligibility table (one row per type: measured, O88 donor-following, O46 ceiling, expired-in-grace, fault-window, owner transfer, API fallback) in the gate definition and in scores.json, build-checked.

3. **E268 couples a scored leaf to another benchmark's record selection.** The AI Coding Daily row for a twice-listed configuration is chosen by the harness of its Bug Hunt record, so a Bug Hunt refresh that changes the E206 largest-sample harness can silently switch the scored AI Coding Daily value (today: DeepSeek V4.1 Flash 1017, DeepSeek Harness over OpenCode) with no change in AI Coding Daily's own data. E334's replay attributes the change, but attribution does not remove the spurious error channel: evidence from benchmark A moves the score reading benchmark B. Why it hurts: exact-variant evidence is the fixed policy core, yet this rule lets a third benchmark's run-count dynamics determine which variant of a leaf is scored. Recommended change: select the AI Coding Daily row by a rule on that board's own data (largest N, then latest test date), keep the Bug-Hunt-harness-matched row as the labelled alternative, and publish both.

4. **Quality is two-thirds one party's runs, with no source-level stability diagnostic.** The max-effort ranking is 66% AA-run and the AI Coding Daily ranking 78%; the draft's own history shows the exposure is real (DeepSWE withdrawal O49, Intelligence Index v5 O132 — each forced a workload migration with no dual run). Cross-checks exist per benchmark (E229, E234), but nothing aggregates them into a source-level statement. Why it hurts: the objective is a public ranking of frontier models; if AA's runs drift, misprice or withdraw, the headline moves with no independent corroboration measure. Recommended change: each release, publish Kendall τ_b on the common panel between the full composite and (a) the renormalised AA-run-only composite and (b) the renormalised independent-only composite, beside the pooled E229/E234 cross-source checks, with a disclosed flag threshold (e.g., τ_b < 0.7).

5. **The E114 drift flag has no consequence path.** The knowledge group is flagged (spread share 8.7% against a nominal 5.3%) and the draft says "informational" — permanently, as far as these parts state. Why it hurts: a persistently mis-sized quality group never triggers any review, so nominal weights can diverge from realized influence indefinitely. Recommended change: a k-consecutive-release trigger (k = 3) that names a pending owner weight-review decision in the release record — no automatic weight change, just a mandatory, visible escalation.

**Low**

6. **A non-discriminating leaf still consumes its full weight.** AA-LCR v1.1 (population SD of z below 3 points) holds 5% of quality in both rankings; it is scored, reported and excluded only from drift counting. Its effect is to compress quality dispersion by roughly its weight, diluting the exchange rate between quality and cost. Recommended change: publish a shadow composite with non-discriminating leaves' weights renormalised away (diagnostic only), as E146/E234-style removal sensitivities already do for benchmarks.

7. **The Terminal-Bench cross-source agreement panels are too small to mean much.** E229 reports τ_b 0.74 on 5 rows (max-effort) and undefined on 1 row (AI Coding Daily). Recommended change: publish the pooled common panel across both rankings' rows as a supplementary diagnostic (the underlying matched model-effort pairs are the same evidence; E277 restricts separability, not source agreement).

8. **Owner decision challenge (O170, live Bug Hunt scale).** New argument: the scale's maxima are set by any run on the live board, any model or harness — i.e., out-of-cohort evidence enters every cohort row's denominator — and U_max is open-ended, so accumulated board growth drifts all z values monotonically downward with no new evidence about any ranked row. O173's ±10% stress is instantaneous, not a cross-release trend statement, and O25 protects only the published record, not a reader comparing releases. Recommended change (not a scale change): publish the (P_max, U_max) time series per release and a frozen-scale shadow z, so cross-release readers can separate scale movement from performance movement.

## 2. Methodology design recommendations

Prioritised; all are implementable from public data and refreshable.

1. **Cost-influence stabilisation (addresses finding 1).** Per basis and ranking, define the published exchange rate λ = 25 × 100·ln2 / ln(c_H/c_L) (value points per cost doubling); print it in every headline next to the nominal 25%. Fix a minimum anchor log-span s_min = ln 50 at the methodology-version level: when the reference panel's span ln(max ref / min ref) + 2·ln2 falls below s_min, widen symmetrically (c_L and c_H moved outward by equal log amounts) so the padding logic and the "references never clip" property are preserved. Any span change beyond 2× between releases is an E177 named swing class with replay. Data source: existing scores.json anchor objects; no new capture needed.

2. **Plan gate enumeration table (addresses finding 2).** A static table in the gate section, mirrored as a build-checked JSON manifest: for each Plan row type (measured; O88 donor-following; O46/O125 provider-derived ceiling; expired-in-grace O118; fault-window O119; owner transfer O4/O7; API fallback O13), three booleans — ranked, in G, anchor-eligible. The build fails if a Plan row's type is not in the table, exactly as E225 fails on an unregistered agent.

3. **Source-concentration diagnostic (addresses finding 4).** Each release, on the declared common panel I: τ_b(full composite, AA-run-only renormalised composite) and τ_b(full, independent-only renormalised composite), plus the count and mean offset of the existing per-benchmark cross-source checks (E229 AA-vs-tbench.ai — whose leaderboard does publish one row per model, agent and resolution rate; E234 AA-vs-Cognition). Publish beside the existing 66%/34% source-share line; flag at τ_b < 0.7. [tbench](https://www.tbench.ai/)

4. **Drift-flag escalation (addresses finding 5).** Extend E114: D_g is compared with its nominal share over a rolling window of releases; the third consecutive flagged release of the same group writes a mandatory "pending owner weight review" entry into the release record (owner decision required to clear it, per the O55 weight-process).

5. **E268 decoupling (addresses finding 3).** Replace the Bug-Hunt-harness key with: largest N among the board's rows for that model and effort, then latest test date, then build-stop on ties (the existing tie rule); the Bug-Hunt-harness-matched row is published as the labelled alternative in `alternates`. Vals' TB 4.0 run shows a single-harness board is the norm; the coupling rule exists only because AI Coding Daily runs mixed harnesses. [vals](https://www.vals.ai/benchmarks/terminal-bench-4)

6. **Non-discriminating-leaf shadow (addresses finding 6).** One extra scores.json diagnostic: ranks and composite SD with weights renormalised over B* (the eligible set E114 already defines), on the common panel.

7. **Bug Hunt scale series (addresses finding 8).** scores.json gains `bugHuntScale` history (P_max, U_max, setting runs, per release) and a frozen-scale shadow z computed at the first captured scale, published never as a score.

8. **Pooled TB agreement panel (addresses finding 7).** A single supplementary τ_b and mean offset over the union of both rankings' matched AA/tbench.ai pairs.
