Medium

Bug Hunt board maxima are asserted exact, which silently breaks the separability premise and applies a lower precision discipline than the neighbouring Omniscience rule. Part 14's E89 asserts the no-shared-factor condition holds in v1 "and Bug Hunt's two scale maxima are fixed release constants (E346), not primitives". But under O170 the denominator of every row's Bug Hunt z (board max planted + board max unplanted) is itself a published board value, and O25 puts rounding of the published record into Θ. If the board's maxima are captured at display precision (rounded), the maxima are one shared uncertain quantity feeding every row's quality — exactly what E147 calls "a shared factor drawn once per scenario [that] links every row it feeds", which the registered v1 kernel compilation (singleton components) and the E89 corner-reduction both assume away. Meanwhile this same part handles Omniscience more carefully: rates are "captured at AA's full stored precision, not as displayed, … and a capture whose rates were rounded would add their propagated half-widths to the tolerance". Why it hurts the objective: ValueRank's labels promise "stable under the published record's reporting precision"; a rounded shared denominator moving all Bug Hunt z's together is a reporting-precision effect the interval outputs and kernel checks would both miss, and E89's per-release assertion would then be asserting something untrue. Recommended change, in order of preference: (a) capture and register the board maxima at full precision (raw counts if published) and record them as exact, mirroring the Omniscience capture rule — the maxima then legitimately stay constants; (b) if only rounded maxima exist, register one shared rounding primitive per maximum and let the E73/E276 shared-factor machinery handle the co-movement (or document a measured bound showing the effect is below reporting precision and reclassify it as an E169 measurement-layer approximation, not part of the E89 premise); (c) in all cases, record a Bug Hunt board-max change as a named bridge cause in the E177 swing attribution, since O170's live denominator shifts every row's z at once.

2. Methodology design recommendations

Prioritised:

Close the shared-denominator gap (finding 1). Full-precision capture of Bug Hunt board maxima where the source allows; otherwise a registered shared primitive so kernel compilation (E147) and the interval model see the coupling. One registry entry and one build check, no recomputation of labels expected.

Ship machine-checkable interval certificates. Parts 13–14 build elaborate certification machinery (E40/E43, E73, E79, E108: exact-rational affine classes, certified branch-and-bound with witness/counterexample storage). The strongest version of this for a public ranking is an external, independent checker: emit each decision's certificate in a small normal form (interval-arithmetic proof steps over the declared primitive domains and constraints) that a ~200-line checker, versioned with the release, validates without trusting the solver. This pattern is established practice — δ-complete solvers such as dReal generate independently checkable proofs from interval branch-and-prune for exactly this reason, and proof-logging/certificate checking is standard in constraint programming. As a cross-validation, a δ-complete decision procedure run on a sampled subset of decisions per release (the classes dReal handles natively — polynomial constraints with bounded boxes — cover most of E108's supported classes) would catch solver bugs the fixtures miss.
