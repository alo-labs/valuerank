1. Findings by severity

Critical

None.

High

Provider-claimed (E233) rows can set bar levels. Part 14's E92 states: "a provider-claimed row that is not provisional still counts in the bar population, since a bar is a displayed-quality level, E192", and part 13's population rule ("the ranking's ranked rows that are not provisional and have a displayed quality") admits them. So a row whose quality rests partly on a provider-claimed leaf — admitted under E233 precisely because its evidence is not trusted for labels — can become the bar setter and move H_1/H_2/H_3 for every other row. This hurts the objective twice over: an overstated provider claim raises H and denies verified rows their cheapest-above-bar labels and scenario-supported labels (bar outputs are a headline view under O2), while an understated one lowers H and can let a row qualify that verified evidence would not support; the E203 displayed-record cheapest and the O143/O172 headline answers also read the shifted level. The row itself "earns no label", but that guard does not extend to the level it sets for others. This is not in the decision ledger (E233 covers competing, not bar-setting) and is not an owner-decision challenge — the population definition is E156/E192. Recommended change: add ¬provider-claimed(i) to the bar-population predicate (the row stays ranked, stays a blocker in B_W, still competes at its own box), applied at the next versioned re-freeze under the existing E197/E207 transition machinery, and publish the with/without ladder as a one-line sensitivity in barCurrency.

Medium

The merged competitor kernel group can understate the worst case. Part 14's E112 registers the competitor role as a single switch group, so the assignment set A = {uniform, arcsine}^G tests only "all competitors uniform" and "all competitors arcsine" (plus candidate/reference groups), and the draft discloses that "independently varying competitor components switch together and are not exhaustively checked". What is new beyond that disclosure: the scenario-supported event is a minimum over competitors, and a mixed assignment (some competitor components arcsine, others uniform) is admissible under the same identification ambiguity yet its frequency is not bounded by the two homogeneous cases — dispersion heterogeneity across competitors has no monotone ordering here, so the published "frequency range across assignments" can read ≥ 90% on both registered assignments while a mixed one falls below. Recommended change: a bounded mixed-assignment diagnostic — enumerate all per-competitor splits when the competitor count is ≤ 6, otherwise a pre-registered random sample of 64 mixed assignments — publishing the minimum frequency beside the range as a diagnostic only; labels and the E112 authorisation rule stay unchanged.

Low

Owner decision challenge (narrow): the O28 residual tolerance hard-codes AA's display precision. The build stops above a 0.05 residual, "half AA's displayed precision", i.e. an assumed 0.1 display. The draft already handles rounded rates but not a change in the index's displayed precision; if AA later displays whole points (or two decimals), the tolerance is respectively too tight (spurious build stops on legitimate rounding) or too loose. Recommended change: derive the tolerance per capture as half the last displayed digit observed on the captured page, recorded in the capture manifest; the O28 policy itself (stop on residual) is untouched.

E171 noise screen can mix evidence vintages. "A bar record without a frozen SD falls back to the setter's current SD" — if the setter's record has changed since the freeze, that current SD belongs to different evidence than the frozen H, pairing a new dispersion with an old level. E351 handles the setter's changed quality but not this fallback path. Recommended change: freeze levelSd at every re-freeze unconditionally (E352) and treat a missing frozen SD as unknown noise ("rounding-stable only") rather than substituting another vintage.

The E183 blocker paragraph buries five owner decisions in one sentence. The O100/O103/O106/O108/O109 register rules sit inside a parenthetical inside the hull definition, a ~300-word sentence. This is an editorial risk rather than a defect: future amendments to the footnote register will be easy to mis-apply where they are nested. Recommended change: split into a "Blocker registry (O94/O100–O109)" subsection with the hull definition referencing it.

2. Methodology design recommendations

Prioritised, all implementable from public data on the existing build:

Bar population = verified-evidence rows only (High finding above). Population filter: ranked ∧ ¬provisional ∧ ¬provider-claimed ∧ displayed quality present; blockers keep competing in B_W. Ship as barPopulationHash in the bar policy object so the change is auditable per freeze, with the excluded setters named.

Bounded mixed-kernel diagnostic (Medium finding above). Cost: at most 2^6 or 64 extra scenario evaluations per label decision, reusing the E77 lower-bound machinery; publish kernelMixedMinFrequency beside the existing range.

Capture-derived O28 tolerance: tolerance = ½ × 10^(−d), where d is the number of displayed decimals on the captured index; recorded per capture. One line of manifest schema.

Unconditional levelSd freeze at each re-freeze (Low 2), removing the vintage-mixing fallback.

Workday-grid diagnostic (E15): the capacity program already solves a 40-hour week; publish M_workday/M_full at 20 h and 60 h as well, from the same solver and calendar, so light-user readers see the sensitivity of the diagnostic to the pre-registered week. O3 (full utilisation stays the scored estimand) is untouched.

Extend E219 observed-range sensitivity to the Plan basis when Plan interval outputs become evaluable; today it recomputes only API bar results, and the Plan branch would be vacuous but should be wired for symmetry before the first Plan row with an identified M_W arrives.
