1. Findings by severity

Critical: None

High

Plan headline is ordered mostly on evidence the methodology itself flags as stale, with no staleness diagnostic on the ordering claim. The Known limitations section states that 21 of 30 max-effort plan rows and 60 of 74 AI Coding Daily plan rows rest on routes flagged possibly superseded or expired, plus 3 and 12 rows resting on fault-window measurements. Disclosure is present, but the official Plan ranking still prints a single ordering in which 70–81% of entries are priced on non-current evidence, and nothing quantifies how much that ordering would move on current evidence alone. I am not challenging the lifecycle stages themselves (O13/O32/O155 are settled); what is missing is a measurement of the headline's exposure to staleness, in the spirit of E287's always-published cross-checks. Concrete change: publish, beside the Plan ranking, a leave-stale-out comparator diagnostic — the τ_b between the official Plan order and the order recomputed with every non-Current route replaced by API fallback (the E142 machinery already computes eligibility stages) — plus the count and identity of rank changes, and a stage-mix count in the table header ("Current: n/N; possibly superseded: n/N; expired in grace: n/N"). This adds no new list, no label and no policy change; it converts a disclosed limitation into a measured one, matching how E224 harness sensitivity and E274 ranking agreement were handled.

Medium

Grid-checked capacity bounds may not enclose continuous-time capacity (soundness of certified M_W). Under E39, time is discretised (default 15 minutes) and sliding windows are enforced as ∀t over grid points. A sliding-window sum over an off-grid start can strictly exceed every grid-aligned window sum when consumption spikes straddle cells (e.g., two bursts in adjacent 15-minute cells that no grid-aligned 5-hour window catches together but an off-grid one does), so a schedule certified as "verified feasible" on the grid can be infeasible under continuous sliding semantics — invalidating the feasible lower bound and, when the two grid bounds coincide, a point-certified capacity. The existing burst fixtures (one hour vs six hours apart) probe only grid-aligned spacing and cannot catch this. Materiality is limited today (no Plan route has an identified M_W), but the formula is v1-normative. Concrete change: adopt a conservative crediting convention — each cell's consumption is counted in every window that intersects the cell — under which grid-checked constraints imply continuous feasibility at zero solver cost; publish the capacity lost to the convention as a measurement-evidence diagnostic (parallel to E76's completion-ratio diagnostic), never as a score.

Low

Calendar-day ageing hides up to ~24 hours of true age at stage boundaries (E314). The draft already documents this ("an observation up to about 24 hours past a boundary can still count in the lower stage"), so the rule is settled; the additive fix is to print each route's elapsed capture-to-cutoff age in hours beside its stage badge. This does not repeat E314's rationale — it changes nothing the rule computes, and it directly serves the same readers the staleness diagnostic above serves.

The weekly SemiAnalysis watch lists X accounts among its sources, but the Known limitations record that the staged automated search cannot reach X (HTTP 402). If the watch runs in the same network conditions, its X leg can never fire, and a new subscription-usage article announced only on X would stall detection until the web/newsletter legs pick it up — with no gate noticing. Concrete change: per-leg reachability logging in the watch output; two consecutive unreachable runs surface as "unresolved" in the E63 active-rules manifest.

The reproducibility treatment of the scenario RNG is mostly right — Python documents a cross-version stability guarantee only for random() itself, while the Beta and normal samplers built on it (used by O142's Jeffreys Beta draws and the clipped normal leaves) are explicitly subject to change — and the draft compensates by recording the Python version per scenario file. What is missing is drift detection, not just recording: a CI fixture that reproduces a pinned draw sequence under the recorded version, and a hash of the scenario output files in the evidence bundle (scenarios.outputHash), so a silent sampler change on a build host is caught rather than archived.
