
## Uncertainty (three separate layers, one scenario engine)

A joint scenario simulation (N = 10,000) is the single engine. **Primitive sampling (E83)**: scenarios draw the primitive variables of the constrained evidence model (E73) under the selected branch and then derive costs, capacities, multiples, quality and value; derived quantities (e.g. Plan cost = C_W / M_W = F / λ under compatible identified transport) are never drawn independently; every scenario must pass the applicable identities and feasibility checks; infeasible combinations are never silently rejected and redrawn unless that conditional sampling law is itself declared; where public evidence supports no joint sampling mechanism, the branch publishes bounds or is unevaluable (fixtures: C/M = F/λ; gross cash changing with consumption under an evidenced payment rule; shared donor and price primitives propagating to every affected row; individually admissible draws that violate a joint constraint). **Cost is drawn too**: published rounding supports reporting-precision draws, and public paired per-task or per-run cost observations for the exact configuration support resampling at their evidenced level; an analyst-selected token-use range belongs only in a named S2 stress branch, and without supporting observations repeatability is reported as "not quantified", never zero and never an invented S0 distribution (E134; today S0 cost draws are reporting precision only, and the build rejects an S0 primitive whose parameter_derivation cites only an analyst convention). **Joint rounding law (E154)**: rounding is a conventional treatment, not an empirical error distribution. The build compiles each connected constraint component of rounded published records (records linked by an accounting identity, e.g. rounded component costs and a rounded aggregate) and gives it a registered joint-law object (constraint_component_id, published field IDs, free source coordinates, joint_law_id, sampler_version, sampling_status). A singleton component keeps its independent uniform kernel (arcsine alternative); a derived quantity is the identity applied to its drawn primitives — a basket cost is its exact components plus each rounded component's own draw, never one uniform on the summed interval; an affine-linked component uses the registered v1 convention, uniform volume on the feasible polytope in its declared free coordinates, sampled only by an explicitly registered exact algorithm within its resource limit; without a registered law, or beyond that limit, the component keeps deterministic bounds and the affected frequencies are withheld as "joint rounding law not evaluated" — feasibility alone never activates sampling (fixture: two rounded components and a rounded aggregate, every draw inside all three reporting intervals and satisfying the identity; equivalent accounting representations give the same derived-cost distribution). In v1 the admitted basket's costs are captured at source precision (AA's per-evaluation cost per task and the Coding Agent Index mean are unrounded) and the quality leaves are separately published records, so every compiled component is a singleton; aggregates (AA's Index total, the Coding Agent Index published sum) are deterministic validation identities, never sampling constraints, and the build fails if a multi-record component lacks a registered law. AA's published cost already embeds AA's cache-pricing protocol (registry: protocol and date), so cache-hit variation is a **named S2 specification-stress branch** around that protocol, not an S0 redraw (E34). **Layer membership (E122)**: S0 and S1 use the primary price rule only; the first-party-list versus cross-provider-median comparison is a named S2 specification branch with shared price primitives and all demand–cost identities kept within the branch; each stochastic primitive carries uncertainty_layer (S0 reporting precision or supported measurement variation under the primary rule / S1 missing evidence or identified transport / S2 alternative price rule, cache policy, quality mapping or weights / bounds-only), validated at build time so a methodological alternative never enters a measurement-labelled stratum. Cost and AA-run leaf values are coupled only as the Dependence rule allows (paired public observations; otherwise dependence stress branches). **Stream allowlist (E145)**: each stream's registered target profile is an explicit allowlist and takes precedence over the generic sampling instructions in this section — a primitive enters a stream only when the profile names its target. For v1: S0 varies only the published records' reporting precision (cost and leaf rounding); S1 adds the declared conventional identification draws (missing-leaf domains; Plan transport and transfer bounds only where identified — none today); S2 core adds hierarchical weight stress; source-supported uncertainty about expected performance (e.g. a fitted-rating confidence interval) or future runs (public run or task resamples) enters only a separately named stream whose documented estimand supports it, never S0–S2. **Published-interval stream (E162, E165)**: v1 has one such stream, **S1T (owner decision O39)** = S1 with every observed leaf redrawn from a normal with its plug-in binomial standard error, 100·√(p̃(1−p̃)/n) on the 0–100 scale (for a leaf whose per-task score lies in [0, 1] but is not binary, such as AutomationBench-AA's partial scores, FrontierCode's tests plus rubric and AI Coding Daily's judged points, p(1 − p) is the largest variance a mean p allows, so its plug-in SD is an upper bound on the independent-task SD), where n is the leaf's published task count and p̃ = (k + ½)/(n + 1) is the Jeffreys proportion for the published score's k = p·n successes, so a 0% or 100% score keeps a positive spread (E241) (FrontierCode 2.0 132, Terminal-Bench 66, AutomationBench-AA 657, AA-LCR 100, Terminal-Bench-Science 70, HLE-Diamond 1,000, CritPt v2 40, Bug Hunt's 105 planted bugs, LiveBench 2.0 Overall as the weighted mean of its seven categories, each a binomial share of its published task count (Long Agentic 18, Reasoning 4, Coding 29, Browser 25, Data Analysis 3, Research 22, Design 25; Reasoning and Data Analysis count their parts, each holding several short questions, so their counts are the wide reading; E264), and AI Coding Daily with n = 6, its graded projects, and p = points ÷ 60, an assumption (E263): six trials of the points share overstate the variance of a mean of six bounded project scores, so this is the wide reading; the narrow reading, n = 60, is published beside each separability figure (today it would move the AI Coding Daily ranking's median threshold from 4.20 to 3.04 API and from 4.39 to 3.15 Plan, with 0 of 14 and 0 of 33 adjacent ranks separable), and a sample SD ÷ √6 of the six project scores would replace both once the board's per-project points are captured), clipped to 0–100; the two Vals boards from Vals' published standard error per record (E256); AA-Omniscience v1.1 from its 3,000 questions scored +1/0/−1, with a = AA's accuracy and h = its hallucination rate × (1 − a), so the index's SD is 100 √((a + h − (a − h)²) / 3,000), halved on the z scale (O28); GDPval-AA, a fitted rating with no task outcomes, from each row's rating interval as AA's model page displays it, read as a 95% normal interval with SD = half-width ÷ 1.96 (an assumption, since AA states no level, so its kernel is marked analyst-derived); the ratings are drawn independently, although a fitted Crowd-BT model's ratings covary through shared matchups, so pairwise rating differences may be over- or under-dispersed (an approximation disclosed, not corrected: the captured model pages carry only each row's own interval). This is the same unstated-level interval E174 keeps out of S1M, admitted here only because O39 asks for a labelled approximation; the wide reading (±26 Elo, the width AA displayed for Claude Opus 5.5 on 2026-10-20, as one SD for every row) is published as a sensitivity (today median API threshold 2.61 against 2.60 in the max-effort ranking, 4.21 against 4.20 in the AI Coding Daily ranking). Tasks are treated as independent and attempts averaged within a task are ignored, so the stream is labelled an independent-task approximation; transfers are off on the Plan basis as in the other core streams, it never authorises a label and it changes no score. **S1M** = S1 plus conventional range sensitivity inside source-documented 95% confidence intervals: Terminal-Bench 4.0 on tbench.ai is the only one captured in v1 (tbench.ai: "The whiskers span the 95% confidence interval"; field accuracy_ci95_half_width, with n_trials). Since O37 the Terminal-Bench leaf is AA's own run, which publishes no interval, so S1M widens only a tbench.ai gap fill; with none today, S1M equals S1 and is published as such, and the active-rules manifest marks the stream dormant (E236). **Captured, level unstated (E174)**: AA's model pages display a per-configuration GDPval-AA v2.1 rating interval (e.g. −26/+26 Elo for Claude Opus 5.5 (Max, Default Fallback) on 2026-10-20) on ratings that AA's methodology says are fitted "to a Crowd-BT model via maximum likelihood estimation" with "confidence intervals using the sandwich estimator", but neither the leaderboard nor the GDPval-AA methodology section states the interval's level (the 95% level and "ties counted as half-wins" on the same page belong to the AA-Briefcase ratings); the capture has carried the interval on every model page since the 2026-10-23 re-capture (O47), and the registry lists it as "captured, level unstated" (scores.json `sourceIntervals`); documentation of the level counts wherever the source publishes it (leaderboard or methodology page). It enters S1M only once its level is documented; until then it is published beside the leaf in each row's GDPval provenance (`ratingInterval`) as a source interval of unstated level and enters no stream but S1T, under O39's labelled approximation above. A confidence interval is not a distribution over the parameter, and the captured documentation does not establish its estimand, interval method, sampling unit or treatment of repeated trials, so S1M draws inside the interval on the z scale under a conventional kernel (uniform, arcsine alternative, never assumed normal, clipped to the leaf's domain, no mass outside the interval: tails are not explored, never invented); **asymmetric intervals (E182)**: when the interval, as published or after clipping to the domain, is not centred on the published point, the kernel puts half its mass on each side of the point (uniform on each side; the arcsine alternative on each side), so the published point stays the median; the point must lie inside the transformed interval (else the build fails), a zero-length side is a point mass at the published point (so a point at an endpoint carries an atom of half the mass there) and a point interval is a point mass, and atoms are published in the kernel object. Its label comes from an **interpretation contract** (source interval with endpoints, confidence level and submission identity; source_estimand; source_interval_method; sampling_unit; repeated_task_treatment; kernel_status source-derived or conventional-within-CI; tail_treatment): "expected-performance uncertainty" is permitted only for a source-derived kernel whose estimand, method, unit and repeat treatment are all documented; otherwise, as today, the stream reads "conventional CI-range sensitivity (endpoint-conditioned; no tail model)". Every other leaf stays at reporting precision (the captured pages carry no interval for any other AA leaf, Bug Hunt's owner publishes run values, captured where published, that no v1 stream uses (E29), and Vals' standard errors enter S1T only, E256); S1M uses the full composite, is labelled "S1 plus conventional range sensitivity inside Terminal-Bench's published 95% confidence interval (one leaf)" while a gap fill carries such an interval and "dormant: equals S1" otherwise (E236), is published beside S1 and never authorises a label (E100), in line with O25 (published confidence intervals are reported only through scenario shares). The build rejects a fitted-rating interval or a run range in S0–S2 and an analyst token-use range in S0 or S1; a run-level bootstrap can never be declared reporting precision; each stream's output publishes its permitted and actually varied targets, and its frequency label is derived from that object, not free text. Within its allowlist, each scenario draws leaf values from their kernels, multiples from their ranges, identification bounds for missing leaves, a **hierarchical** weight draw that preserves the nominal means — group weights ~ Dirichlet(100 × (0.60, 0.20, 0.10, 0.10)), within-group weights ~ Dirichlet(50 × nominal within-group proportions), except that a one-leaf group (Reliability, AA-Omniscience alone) takes within-group weight exactly one with no draw (E99; fixture: the leaf's weight equals its group share in every scenario); intent is modest perturbation around 60/20/10/10, not alternative buyer preferences (realised per-leaf CV published), and (as named stress-test branches, not random events) source removal, the GDPval mapping alternative, cache-rate variation, the Bug Hunt harness band and the owner assumptions on/off; shared multiples and price lists are drawn once per scenario. Strata are cumulative and computable from the registry: **S0 measurement** = renormalised composite over observed leaves with the reporting-precision leaf and cost draws its profile allows (E145); **S1** = S0 + identification draws (missing-leaf bounds, attempts bias, Plan transport and transfer bounds); **S2 core branch** = S1 + hierarchical weight draws (transfers off, all sources, linear GDPval map); other S2 branches are published separately with explicit reasons when unevaluable. **Transfers off (E167)** is selected-evidence removal: on the Plan basis each row's primary selected route–measurement pair stays fixed, and a row whose pair rests on an owner assumption (an owner-named transfer or an O46 provider quota ceiling, which the build grades "assumption" alike) is unevaluable in the owner-assumptions-off streams (S1 core, S2 core, S1M and S1T), listed with its reason and left out of the declared panel before the draws — never reselected and never replaced by API cost; reselection among evidence without an owner assumption is the measured-only view (E113), and S0 describes the displayed record, owner assumptions included.

**Draw-kernel objects (E25)**: every sampled input carries interval_interpretation, kernel_family, kernel_parameters (incl. truncation and mode where relevant), parameter_derivation, dependency_id and alternative_kernel_id. Hierarchy: public paired observations → clustered resampling; rounding interval → uniform; published estimate with documented standard error and an applicable approximation → that declared approximate distribution; interval endpoints only → an explicitly conventional kernel (uniform on the interval) published with an endpoint-heavy alternative — the arcsine law, Beta(½, ½) rescaled to the same interval (E119) — (scenario outputs shown under both); no defensible convention → bounds-only, and no scenario-share claim may depend on that input. A "95% interval" is never assumed normal, except in S1T, where owner decision O39 reads GDPval-AA's displayed rating interval as a 95% normal interval and labels it an assumption; Terminal-Bench intervals and AA fitted-rating intervals otherwise keep their source interpretations. An unspecified kernel fails the scenario build. **Scenario targets (E58)**: every stochastic input also carries scenario_target (expected benchmark performance / future deployment run / reporting precision / specification convention), sampling_level (task / repository / run / fitted parameter / shared factor), the public evidence supporting that target and level, and the interpretation label permitted beside the resulting scenario share; uncertainty about expected performance is kept separate from predictive run-to-run variation, and only interpretations the public evidence supports are published.

Results are published **per stratum** (S0, S1, S2; transfers on/off; leave-AA-out), never averaged into one figure, as scenario shares (e.g. share of scenarios with rank ≤ 3, on the frontier, qualifying at a bar; pairwise ordering shares), labelled as scenario frequencies, not probabilities. **Stream events (E161)**: each published frequency names its event and the quality equation that event uses — S0 "observed-only score frontier frequency" (Q̄_i, observed leaves renormalised), S1 "full-composite frontier frequency under conventional missing-evidence draws" (nominal-weight full composite), S2 "full-composite frontier frequency under missing-evidence and weight-stress draws"; F_i^(s)(v) = 1[∄ j ≠ i: C_j(v) ≤ C_i(v) ∧ Q_j^(s)(v) ≥ Q_i^(s)(v), one strict]; rank and bar events carry the same field, an S0-to-S1 difference is reported as a change of event target plus added identification draws, never as uncertainty alone, and frequencies are compared directly only when event, target and panel match (fixture: two configurations with different coverage whose observed-only order differs from an admissible full-composite order produce distinct S0 and S1 frontier events). **Rank events (E96)**: every published rank-frequency series has a declared panel I, fixed before the draws and named with its size (E266: the ranking's rows scored on its main workload; a subset-workload row gets no scenario share, and the scenario tables name such rows beside each ranking that has them, so a subset-workload leader is never shown as absent from rank 1 without the reason); a branch exclusion changes the declared panel and never the denominator scenario by scenario; ranks are competition ranks, r_i(s) = 1 + #{j ∈ I∖{i}: V_j(s) > V_i(s)}, as in the displayed table, so ties can put more than k rows at rank ≤ k (disclosed beside each top-k series); pairwise results publish win, tie and loss frequencies f_ij^>, f_ij^=, f_ij^< separately; baseline-versus-branch frequency changes use the E65 common panel on both sides, and frequencies on different workload objects keep the label "cross-workload ordering not identified" (O17). **Monte Carlo precision (E72)**: every published scenario frequency carries its Monte Carlo standard error √(p̂(1 − p̂)/N), separate from the substantive layers; a frequency whose repeated-look-adjusted interval (Bonferroni over the 16 declared looks, E127 — not an anytime-valid confidence sequence) crosses a decision threshold (90%) triggers pre-registered expansion in batches of 10,000 up to 160,000 scenarios, and if it still crosses, the label is "scenario threshold unresolved"; small Monte Carlo differences never create extra ranking distinctions. **Sampling validity (E135)**: every frequency certified by E77 uses IID scenario replicates from one fixed declared law per event and kernel assignment — dependence required by the evidence model stays within each replicate, random numbers may be reused across alternative specifications, but the law, event definition, competitor panel and assignment never adapt within a certified stream; any other sampling design publishes descriptive frequencies only, unless it supplies its own matching error calculation. **Sequential rule (E77)**: looks at N = 10,000, 20,000, …, 160,000 (16 looks); at each look an exact equal-tailed binomial (Clopper–Pearson) interval with **per-look error allocation δ = 0.05/16** (confidence level 1 − δ = 0.996875, δ/2 in each tail; L = Beta⁻¹(δ/2; k, n−k+1), or 0 at k = 0; U = Beta⁻¹(1 − δ/2; k+1, n−k), or 1 at k = n; in SciPy the argument is confidence_level = 1 − δ, never δ — E150) per published event, each certificate recording per_look_error, confidence_level, tail_allocation, k, n, L and U (regression fixture: the closed-form endpoints (δ/2)^(1/n) at k = n and 1 − (δ/2)^(1/n) at k = 0) (the event family and its size are declared in the release manifest; the bound holds per event and is not corrected across the candidates a bar lists, which the label table states beside the candidates, E198a); the 90% label is met when the lower bound ≥ 0.90, not met when the upper bound < 0.90, otherwise the run continues to the next look or ends "scenario threshold unresolved" at the cap. The guarantee is per event, not simultaneous across the family; the plug-in standard error stays a separate descriptive quantity. Overlap is not treated as transitive tie groups. Layers:

- **Measurement** (taxonomy, E169): reporting precision, source-supported estimation uncertainty and repeatability are distinct targets. Active v1 streams quantify reporting precision in S0 and task sampling in S1T (O39); S1M's conventional CI-range sensitivity applies only to a tbench.ai Terminal-Bench gap fill, so it is dormant and no source confidence interval enters any v1 stream today except GDPval-AA's displayed rating interval, which S1T reads under O39's labelled approximation (E236); other estimation uncertainty and repeatability (other published intervals, task- or run-clustered resampling) are not quantified.
- **Identification**: missing-data bounds; Plan transport (M_W given the measured multiple) and owner-assumption bounds. **Plan uncertainty split (E12)**: each route stores a measurement object (receipts, meter, repeat-measurement uncertainty of M_observed — drawn only in a separately named measurement stream where public repeat measurements support it, otherwise "not quantified"; no Plan measurement of a cohort configuration publishes repeats today, E145) and a separate transport object (W hash, quota schema, mechanism, transport bounds; drawn from S1). Where public evidence cannot separate them the route is labelled "measurement and transport not separable" and its S0 result is not described as target-workload support.
