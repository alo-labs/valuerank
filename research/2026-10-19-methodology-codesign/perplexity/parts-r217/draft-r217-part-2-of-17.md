For each bar position and basis it publishes its displayed-record cheapest row beside each leading ranking's answer at the same position (each ranking's bar levels are its own), with whether they differ (scores.json `v3Tracks.common.leadingComparison`); it is an alternative-ranking answer comparison, not a coverage effect: the two answers differ in leaf set, quality formula, bar level, workload and eligible rows at once, so a difference cannot be attributed to the wider coverage alone, and quality levels are never compared across the two formulas (reviewer 90). **Admission per ranking (owner decision O57, implementing E254)**: O15 and E140 admit a component when its exact cost covers two-thirds of "the cohort"; with two rankings this is two-thirds of the ranking's own cohort rows, because each ranking is scored on its own rows and anchors. - **Full benchmark set (owner decisions O36, O38)**: a cohort configuration is ranked in a ranking only when it has every leaf of that ranking for the exact configuration, so every row of a ranking is scored on the same benchmarks at that ranking's owner-set weights (O55, as amended for the max-effort ranking by O61). A leaf comes, in order, from the source the leaf names (AA; the Bug Hunt owner; Vals AI, LiveBench or AI Coding Daily for the leaves those parties run), from a newer capture of that source, from another party that ran the same benchmark version on the exact configuration (tbench.ai for Terminal-Bench, O37; Cognition's own FrontierCode 2.0 board for FrontierCode, owner decision O153, 2026-11-01: read at the exact model and effort through a registered map, one row per configuration, never best-of across efforts, and the build stops when a configuration without AA's run has an exact Cognition row but no fill), then from the model provider's own published result, labelled a provider claim; nothing is transferred across efforts or versions. Each gap fill is published with its source type, publisher, evaluated configuration and link. A leaf still missing after an exhaustive search needs a search log entry (configuration, leaf, sources checked, result) dated at most 7 days before the release cutoff, or the build fails; the configuration is then listed with its missing leaves and the search, never silently dropped, and gets no rank and no value score in that ranking (beside it, it shows only O41's indicative value, labelled not ranked and not comparable). This applies to a required frontier family too (O38): the reconciliation gate accepts such a logged exclusion as the only way a frontier family goes unranked, and the search is repeated each release. - **Counts, defined once (per ranking)**: a **cohort row** of a ranking is a cohort configuration ranked in it, a subset of the cohort configurations C_t (plotted and retained), which are never called rows of a ranking (reviewer 93); a **scored row** is a cohort row with a value score and a rank; a **bar population row** is a non-provisional cohort row with a displayed quality. Today the max-effort ranking has 9 cohort rows, 9 scored rows and a bar population of 9, and the AI Coding Daily ranking 20, 20 and 20 (no ranked row is provisional or without a cost). Normative text says "cohort rows" for this population. The panels are different populations and the text never states them equal unless their member sets are. The cohort size N of a ranking, counting every cohort row of it (its ranked configurations, not the whole cohort C_t) whether or not it carries a cost, is the denominator of its admission threshold ⌈2N/3⌉ (today N = 9 and 20, so thresholds 6 and 14). The bar population counts every non-provisional ranked row with a displayed quality (every leaf of the ranking); cost is not required, and the scored-rows ladder is published beside it (E243).
- Otherwise: each row publishes its inclusion route (on the rendered frontier, near it, or retained one release after leaving them, O145), inclusion never depends on a score, and any further narrowing or widening of the cohort is an owner decision taken as a versioned migration, as O51 was. **Frontier-families population (E187, E199)**: the frontier-families anchor diagnostic rebuilds each basis's anchors and exchange rate from the cohort rows that carry a frontier or near-frontier inclusion reason, and the frontier-families bar (E199) recomputes the bar over the non-provisional rows of that population and publishes the cheapest-above-the-bar result at that level beside the primary one; neither replaces the frozen anchors or bar. An O145 retained row carries the "left cohort" reason instead: it is a cohort row (ranked, searched and listed like any other) but not plotted at this release, so both diagnostics leave it out and name it (scores.json `diagnostics.frontierAnchors.retainedExcluded`, reviewer 79), and the frontier-families bar and anchors are a retained-row removal diagnostic conditional on the published workload and component admissions, not the ranking without retention: removing the retained rows could also change admission (two-thirds of the ranking's rows), the workload and the reference panel, which this diagnostic holds fixed (reviewer 90). The published summary names the frontier-families bar beside the headline bar. **One row per family (E205)**: because O51 ranks every plotted on/near effort, a family with many plotted efforts weighs more in the ladder and the anchors. A second diagnostic therefore recomputes the ladder and the anchors from each family's primary row alone (E193, E251, below), for the families whose primary is ranked in the ranking at hand. It publishes the ladder beside the frozen one, and the rebuilt anchors' exchange rates and rank changes on both bases (scores.json `diagnostics.familyPrimary`); a family whose primary is excluded is absent from it. Scores never change.

Under O51 the family's other plotted on/near efforts are ranked in their own right, each in every ranking whose set it has.
