# ValueRank methodology — draft r223

*Where prose and the Normative definitions differ, the Normative definitions govern. Every rule tagged with an owner (O) or editor (E) number is normative wherever it appears; between two tagged rules an owner decision governs an editor decision, and a rule that amends another governs it (E191); an editor record never amends or overrides an owner decision; between two editor rules where neither amends the other, the later-numbered record governs, and finding such a conflict is a defect corrected in the next revision. Each E number names one decision record; a record that holds several rules labels them, and an amendment cites the record it amends. The valuation outcome (4) once tagged E198 is E202.*

## Objective and claim scope

AA's Pareto frontier of Intelligence Index vs. total USD cost to run its Intelligence Index does not answer the question ValueRank exists for: **which frontier AI model is actually lowest cost for the value it delivers in practice**, once (a) subscription-plan economics are considered and (b) benchmarks geared to real software-engineering work are weighed alongside general capability. ValueRank serves two buyers, ranked separately: **API buyers** (pay per token, incl. third-party agents) and **Plan buyers** (provider subscription apps/CLIs: Claude Code, Codex, ChatGPT, SuperGrok…).

Claim scope: "value among the configurations ranked in one ranking (configurations AA plots on, or within the near gap of, its rendered frontier, plus those retained one release after leaving them (O145), that have that ranking's full benchmark set; see Cohort), under that ranking's published benchmark mix and evidence". On the Plan basis a frontier or bar claim also needs identified routes (E61, E67); without them the Plan ranking is exploratory arithmetic, not a claim. ValueRank publishes two rankings (owner decision O53): the **max-effort ranking**, which leads (site and README default, O54), and the **AI Coding Daily ranking**. Each has its own leaf set and weights (O55), anchors, bars, scenario run, basket admission and diagnostics (E253, E254); values, ranks and claims compare within one ranking only, never across the two. Outside the current frontier and near-frontier cohort, only a configuration retained for one release under O145 may stay eligible, and benchmark evidence alone never creates eligibility (O51). Every ranked row is a **configuration** (model version, effort, fallback, route), with a compact configuration ID and an expandable per-leaf execution manifest; tables and claims say "configuration", not "model" (E78). Not a market-wide optimum across all configurations and workloads.

## Source contract (versioned registry)

**Evidence bundle (E159)**: each release is (method version, registry, evidence bundle, extractor versions, outputs). The bundle lists every captured artifact the build reads — source payloads or permitted captures, committed with the release — with its content hash, capture time and extractor, plus a bundle hash; every scored observation records its artifact, field locator and published value. Rebuilding from the bundle at the pinned publication clock must reproduce the admitted components, route selection, anchors, scores and deterministic interval statuses without consulting live pages (and the scenario outputs under the recorded RNG, seed and draw order); where redistribution is restricted, the permitted capture is retained and its hash and locator published. Machine-readable registry per release: AA chart identity, filters and snapshot timestamp (current: "Intelligence Index vs. Cost to Run Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index", x-axis "Cost to Run Intelligence Index (USD, Log Scale)", unit "USD to run all Intelligence Index evaluations", AA Intelligence Index v5.0 (published around 2026-10-27; O132 re-captured every AA source under it and moved the cutoff, O65), captured 2026-11-01T05:53:05Z for that release cutoff, the comparison baseline being the previous capture (2026-10-30T22:15:18Z), also under Index v5.0, whose local date is 2026-10-31); **frontier membership extraction (E195)**: membership source = the rendered Pareto SVG path's vertices matched to the plotted circle centres (tolerance 0.5 px; today all 18 vertices matched at 0 px, as they did at the 2026-10-17 capture), the capture procedure (public chart and tooltips only) and its version; the build fails unless every captured frontier point sits on a path vertex, one point per vertex, and the path rises strictly in chart coordinates (cost and Index both increasing). Two cases are kept apart. Overlapping circles off the line are separated at capture by hovering each and recording the resolution, as the 2026-10-17 capture did for GLM-5.2 and GPT-5.5 (at the current capture every circle returned a distinct tooltip at a 1600 px viewport, so no pair needed resolving). Two circles at one vertex (an exact cost-and-Index tie) are separated the same way; if hovering cannot separate them, the vertex check fails and the build stops until the owner records a decision. Rounded tooltip values tie (today GPT-6 Astra (high), GPT-6 Astra (xhigh) and GPT-6.1 Sol (max) all display 55) and are never used to recompute or check membership; the near rule's rounded-Index gaps are measured against these points; cost field, unit and accounting (as AA defines it, including AA's cache-pricing protocol and its date); per model: version, effort, fallback, serving route and per-benchmark stack; per benchmark: version, raw metric, **pinned source field**, population, attempts, aggregation, source revision/commit, observation and retrieval dates; explicit missingness reason (not evaluated / incompatible variant / no public evidence / withdrawn). **Withdrawn benchmark (E250)**: AA withdrew DeepSWE v1.1 from its Coding Agent Index (O49); its chart and per-configuration results stay in the raw data as "withdrawn by AA, not scored", and no DeepSWE result enters quality, sampling noise, cohort membership or any diagnostic.
