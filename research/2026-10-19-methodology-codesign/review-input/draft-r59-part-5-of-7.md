- **Workload cross-check (mandatory, diagnostic)**: per benchmark b (DeepSWE; Terminal-Bench 4.0), a common exact-configuration panel I_b; d_ib = mean cost per attempted task ÷ resolved fraction (∞ when zero, with uncertainty shown). **Same-execution contract (E68)**: numerator and denominator share one observation key (model, effort, fallback, harness and version, task support, deployment policy); for Terminal-Bench the resolved fraction is that of the AA-priced execution (AA's Index Terminal-Bench 4.0 score), never the tbench.ai quality leaf; for DeepSWE both come from AA's DeepSWE execution; a row without a matching pair is unpriced for this diagnostic. Paired task-level costs and outcomes, where public, are resampled jointly. Kendall τ-b between basket-cost rank and d_ib rank within I_b, separately per benchmark (never pooled); task-cluster resampling where task-level results exist, otherwise a model-row bootstrap labelled as matched-set sensitivity; below n = 8 rows in I_b the statistic is suppressed. **Pre-registered threshold (E41)**: a headline warning when the point estimate τ_b < 0.5 (a policy choice, not a statistical fact); "uncertain agreement" when the resampling interval spans 0.5; the exact matched configuration panel is published beside the statistic.

Shared dependencies move together (one multiple, price update or benchmark version affects all its rows jointly).

## Normative definitions (executable specification)

- **Coverage and scores.** p_i = Σ_{b∈O_i} w_b. Displayed observed-only quality Q̄_i = Σ_{b∈O_i} w_b z_ib / p_i. Full-composite score Q_i(θ) = Σ_{b∈O_i} w_b z_ib(θ) + Σ_{b∉O_i} w_b u_ib, u_ib ∈ [0,100]. **Formal bounds** (E35): Q_i^L = min_{θ∈Θ} Q_i(θ) and Q_i^U = max_{θ∈Θ} Q_i(θ) over the joint admissible set Θ below (measurement intervals of observed leaves and u_ib ∈ [0,100] for missing leaves); "missing at 0/100 at the point estimate" is not the bound. S0 uses Q̄_i; S1/S2 use the full-composite equation. Any sampling law for wholly missing leaves is a declared scenario convention (with one published alternative), never evidence; formal bounds are distribution-free.
- **Joint admissible set Θ (for interval outputs: frontier and bar).** Fixed: nominal weights, primary price rule, frozen route, linear GDPval map (E1). **Target (owner decision O25)**: interval outputs protect the **published record** — the benchmark results and costs as published for each exact configuration (for cost: the published deployment-equivalent cost of the fixed workload). They are robust to the record's reporting precision and to missing evidence, not to run-to-run noise. Benchmark repeatability and fitted-parameter uncertainty (published confidence intervals, run-to-run ranges) are outside the S0–S2 shares and are reported only by separately named streams whose documented estimand supports them (E145, E155; in v1, S1M as a conventional range sensitivity inside Terminal-Bench's published interval, E162, E165); token-use and price-basis variation enter only named S2 specification branches. **Uncertainty descriptions (E155)** are generated from the stream manifests and printed beside every uncertainty output (v1: S0 "published-record reporting-precision scenarios"; S1 "S0 plus conventional missing-evidence scenarios"; S2 core "S1 plus hierarchical weight stress"; S1M "S1 plus conventional range sensitivity inside Terminal-Bench's published 95% confidence interval"; other benchmark repeatability and fitted-parameter uncertainty "not quantified in v1 scenario shares"); a release-text lint rejects any statement that a source interval or run range enters scenario shares unless an active stream's allowlist admits that target. Variable in Θ: rounding intervals of published values at their published precision, missing-leaf and missing-component domains (identification), identified M_W ranges and other structural bounds (e.g. deployment-policy bias bounds). **Constraint basis (O19 under O25)**: each uncertainty object records constraint_basis (rounding of the record / published interval / public repeated runs / structural bound / analyst assumption / score domain), estimand, randomness_unit, constraint_target, coverage_claim and supporting_public_evidence (E46), and separates observed_statistic from target_parameter. **Target-compatibility gate (E49)**: an interval narrows a Θ variable only when its documented constraint_target is that published-record variable (or a public, justified mapping connects them); published confidence intervals and run ranges target other estimands and therefore never enter Θ; a full score domain expresses the absence of a narrower admissible constraint on a missing value. Analyst-assumed allowances never enter Θ. **Completeness (E11)**: every input, whatever its weight, enters Θ through a documented constraint or its full domain; an input with neither makes the affected output "unevaluable" for that row. Missing repeatability evidence never becomes zero uncertainty. Published benchmark intervals are consumed with their stated interpretation. Shared constraints (prices, donor measurements, task/run observations, quota pools) move jointly. With transfers off, a Plan route that depends entirely on an owner-authorised transfer is "unevaluable" in interval outputs (never silently replaced by API cost). Tests: widening Θ cannot move a row from "possible" to "definite" or create a confirmed result; evidence loss is tested in two stages (E38) — the result within R_W may change when a competitor leaves R_W, but the selected-cohort extension can never strengthen.
- **Plan measurement vs transport.** M_observed = eligible API-valued measured consumption ÷ gross cash paid (the measured workload). **M_W** ∈ [L, U] = the multiple for the scored workload W (O15 basket), bound to W's hash, the release price-list version and the quota-schema version; transport evidence for another workload qualifies only if its capacity model or conservative bound also covers W, with its identification mechanism recorded: direct compatible-workload measurement / publicly evidenced additive capacity model / evidenced transport bound / unidentified. The Plan headline score uses M_W where identified, else M_observed (owner-named transfers as labelled assumptions; O16 flag). **Scalar convention (E74)**: a set-identified M_W ∈ [L, U] enters the displayed score at its lower bound L (the "lower-capacity-bound scoring convention", not an expected capacity), also when building the Plan anchor object; the joint Plan cost interval, the value-score interval and common-panel rank sensitivity under the upper-bound convention are published beside it. **Numerator eligibility (E75)**: eligible API-priced consumption is delivered model execution under the declared deployment policy, including policy-permitted retries; failed or rejected requests, platform overhead and evaluation-only repetitions are excluded. **Delivery is not outcome (E97)**: "failed request" means no delivered model execution (transport error, rejection before execution); delivered, policy-permitted execution stays eligible whether the task passes or fails, and a model-generated refusal is delivered execution — the same support as the API basket, whose cost covers every attempt regardless of success, so task failure is never penalised twice. Each record stores request_delivery_status, task_outcome, deployment_policy_role, token_observability, api_basket_treatment, plan_numerator_treatment and eligibility_reason, and U = Σ_r e_r p·t_r with e_r set by delivery and policy, never by outcome (fixtures: delivered pass and delivered fail both included; a permitted retry after a failed attempt — both included; rejected before execution — excluded; inseparable overhead — bounded or unidentified). Each measured consumption record carries execution status, deployment-policy role, token observability, eligibility (included / excluded / bounded) and evidence; where receipts cannot separate eligible from ineligible consumption, a supported bound is carried or the transport is unidentified (never a deduction from cohort averages). **Workload-demand evidence (E44)**: additive-capacity transport needs, per configuration and workload version, demand objects with machine-checkable units (E94): t_ie = category-k tokens per deployment-equivalent task of component e (input, cache read, cache write, reasoning, output); d_ie = n_e × t_ie per component pass, or a directly evidenced component-pass vector with its aggregation operator where the published aggregation is not an ordinary per-task mean (e.g. the Coding Agent Index's 303 × pooled category-token means); and d_iW = Σ_{e∈W} d_ie per complete workload pass, the only demand object the capacity solver accepts (fixture: a per-task vector supplied where a workload-pass vector is required is rejected by its units even when its entries satisfy another identity); each with execution stack, task support, category definitions, source precision and cache-policy compatibility, plus evidence that the capacity mechanism covers the relevant context lengths, tool use and effort (or conservative bounds). Where only partial token information is public, d_iW ranges over the feasible set D_iW and capacity is optimised over it: M_W^L = min_{d∈D, (a,B)∈A} M(d, a, B), M_W^U = max over the same set. A dollar total alone never identifies d. **Demand–cost reconciliation (E84)**: the demand object records aggregation_operator, task_attempt_support, category_telemetry_support, deployment_equivalent_multiplier, cost_reconciliation_residual and compatibility_status, and must reconcile to the scored cost — p_release · d_ie = C_i,e for every component and p_release · d_iW = C_i,W under compatible category definitions and the same aggregation and telemetry-support convention (for the Coding Agent Index, 303 × compatible pooled category-token means); a vector is never rescaled to force agreement; incompatible supports leave this transport mechanism unidentified without changing the displayed O27 cost or its completeness; without a demand object this transport mechanism is unidentified (the displayed score stays under O16). A feasible schedule certifies attainability conditional on its demand and quota inputs, not those inputs. **Plan interval outputs use only an identified M_W**; otherwise the row's Plan cost is "unidentified" for those outputs (it stays scored and ranked). Cohort spread or arbitrary bands never convert "unidentified" into a bound.
- **Frontier (E21).** For a basis and workload W, over the evaluable set R_W = ranked rows with FullCost(i, W) (and, on the Plan basis, identified M_W; subset-cost, partial-component, API-fallback and Plan-unidentified rows are listed as unevaluable): j ≻_θ i ⇔ C_j(θ) ≤ C_i(θ) ∧ Q_j(θ) ≥ Q_i(θ) with at least one strict, using full-composite Q. **Competitor universe (E183)**: B_W holds the rows whose AA cost is on a price not in force at V (E170 outcome 3) that would otherwise belong to R_W; each enters with its cost domain the hull of its published record and its current-tariff bound — an outer admissible domain, not a sampling distribution, with the row's other constraints kept — and on the Plan basis only where its own Plan route is interval-eligible on its basis (being a blocker never identifies a multiple). Competitors range over U_W = R_W ∪ B_W; labels and memberships are awarded only to rows of R_W, and a row of B_W earns none (O29). DefiniteFrontier(i) ⇔ i ∈ R_W ∧ ∀θ∈Θ ∄ j∈U_W∖{i}: j ≻_θ i; PossibleFrontier(i) ⇔ i ∈ R_W ∧ ∃θ∈Θ ∄ j∈U_W∖{i}: j ≻_θ i. A blocker whose domain cannot be constructed makes every claim it could affect "unresolved", never leaving it out. Fixtures: a blocker that dominates a candidate at its published cost but not at its current-tariff cost, and the reverse, each leave the candidate not definite; a cheaper, possibly qualifying blocker prevents a confirmed cheapest; the blocker itself receives no label; adding a blocker never strengthens another row's label; an unidentified or basis-incompatible Plan multiple stays out of the Plan decisions for a blocker too. Shared dependencies are kept. Scenario frontier frequencies are a separate output.
- **Cheapest above a bar.** **Bar-selection policy (E156)**: each bar's reference identity comes from a versioned policy object recorded with the anchor version — bar_policy_id and version, selection mode, selection cutoff, population rule, order, position, tie rule, empty-population rule, lifetime, the population and the selected reference IDs — and is selected before any bar outcome is computed. The v1 policy ("median-definite-frontier", version 1): at anchor freeze, the population is the non-provisional configurations on the API main-workload definite frontier (interval outputs), ordered by displayed quality ascending then configuration ID, and the reference is the lower median; an empty population pins no bar and the bar is published as unavailable; the identity is fixed for the anchor version (stable across releases) and replaced only by a versioned re-freeze that publishes old- and new-bar results on the common evaluable panel as a reference-policy transition, distinct from any configuration's quality or cost change (fixtures: reordering the input rows leaves the reference unchanged; selection never computes a bar outcome; an empty or all-provisional population pins no bar; a re-freeze publishes the transition). **Reference object (E52)**: each bar has its own reference-quality object; BarEvaluable(r) ⇔ r's quality expression and its constraints are evaluable (cost or Plan eligibility not required). Qualification is evaluated against r whenever BarEvaluable(r); r competes only if it independently belongs to R_W; the pinned identity survives cost-evidence loss and is never silently substituted; a bar whose reference quality is not evaluable is unevaluable. Fixtures: a reference with valid quality but missing API cost, and one with valid quality but unidentified Plan transport, both still define the bar. Over R_W and a pinned reference r: i ∈ R_W, competitors j ∈ U_W (E183): **confirmed qualification** ⇔ min_{θ∈Θ}[Q_i(θ) − Q_r(θ)] ≥ 0; **possible qualification** ⇔ max_{θ∈Θ}[Q_i(θ) − Q_r(θ)] ≥ 0; **scenario qualification** = share of scenarios with Q_i(s) ≥ Q_r(s). **Confirmed cheapest** (interval, E38): i qualifies confirmed, is not provisional, and for every j ∈ U_W∖{i} that can possibly qualify, max over {θ ∈ Θ: Q_j(θ) ≥ Q_r(θ)} of [c_i(θ) − c_j(θ)] < 0 (strict; i never competes with itself). **Scenario-supported cheapest**: i is not provisional and, in ≥ 90% of scenarios s of the **authorising branch** (E100: stratum S1 core — the full-composite quality equation with identification draws for missing leaves, nominal weights, transfers off, primary kernels; the label is never issued from S0's observed-leaves renormalisation or from other branches), i qualifies in s and is strictly cheaper than every j ∈ R_W∖{i} qualifying in that same s (the simultaneous event; a row undefined in s makes the event unevaluable, never a win). **Kernel checks (E112, E121, E132, E133; one governing definition, consolidated by E137)**: within the S1 core branch, the label is authorised only by its event's kernel-check object — (1) primitive dependency components, induced by shared primitives and joint sampling mechanisms and **compiled per decision and stream from the primitives that stream actually varies (E147)** — a fixed shared quantity links nothing, a shared factor drawn once per scenario links every row it feeds; the release publishes the compiled components and their hash, role groups linked by one component switch kernels together, and the build fails when the compiled graph differs from the registered kernel-check object (v1 registers singleton components: multiples and price lists are fixed inputs in every v1 stream); (2) kernel-switch groups, which in the registered v1 design are the decision roles candidate, reference and competitors, every component mapped to exactly one group and groups merged when roles share a component (e.g. the candidate is the reference), so independently varying competitor components switch together and are not exhaustively checked; (3) the assignment set A = {uniform, arcsine}^G over the G non-empty switch groups, primitives without a conventional alternative keeping their evidenced law; (4) the published assignment list with its hash, the roles touched, the coverage and each assignment's status. The label is authorised exactly when the row is UniqueLabelEligible and every assignment in A completed with its E77 lower bound ≥ 0.90, and it reads "supported under all registered role-group kernel checks", never implying per-component or broader distributional robustness. Outcomes stay distinct: a check not completed (including an exceeded pre-registered resource limit) withholds the label; an assignment still crossing 90% at the cap reads "scenario threshold unresolved"; a primary pass with a failing registered alternative reads "primary-kernel frequency ≥ 90%; kernel-sensitive"; otherwise "not scenario-supported". The kernel set is frozen before labels are examined, and the frequency range across assignments is published (never its minimum as a probability). Fixtures: candidate equals reference; the reference also competes; several independent competitor components mapped to one competitor group (the build enumerates group assignments, never component-wise ones); a primitive without a conventional alternative; 70% coverage with one missing 10% leaf in each of three groups, observed leaves at 100, against a fully observed reference at 78 — the candidate qualifies when U₁ + U₂ + U₃ ≥ 80, about 91.5% of uniform draws but about 86.7% of arcsine draws, so no label. Other strata and branches publish the same event as a frequency only (S0: "observed-leaves qualifying-and-cheapest frequency"), never as the label (fixture: a non-provisional row with 70% coverage whose observed leaves all score 100 against a fully observed reference at 80 — S0 qualification is 100%, while the full-composite label is unsupported). **Decision target signature (E100)**: every interval or scenario label carries quality_equation (full composite / observed leaves), cost_workload_hash, basis, reference_object_id, scenario_stratum, specification_branch, route_selection_version, candidate_panel_id, kernel_version and scenario_target_profile_id (E143), and a label is valid only with the signature its definition names. **Target profiles (E143)**: every scenario stream declares its target profile — published-record reporting precision; expected benchmark performance (only where the source supports that estimand); future-run prediction (only with public run- or task-level evidence); explicitly conventional identification of missing evidence — separately from its S0/S1/S2 layer; the build validates that every primitive's target and sampling level fit the stream's profile or an explicitly defined mixed-target construction, which then carries a descriptive label and never inherits a stronger interpretation; kernel alternatives change the sampling convention, never the target. In v1, S0 is reporting precision only, S1 adds conventional identification of missing leaves, and S2 adds the weight-specification stress. **One eligibility predicate (E92)**: UniqueLabelEligible(i) = i is a bar competitor ∧ ¬provisional(i), used by both unique-cheapest labels; provisional rows still compete and can block another row's label, and their qualifying-and-cheapest scenario frequencies and K_r membership are published without a unique label. Ties are never broken: equal-cost qualifiers prevent a unique label. **Possible-cheapest set (E45)**: i ∈ K_r ⇔ i ∈ R_W ∧ ∃θ ∈ Θ: Q_i(θ) ≥ Q_r(θ) ∧ ∀ j ∈ U_W∖{i}: [Q_j(θ) < Q_r(θ) ∨ C_i(θ) ≤ C_j(θ)] — rows that are cheapest (ties allowed) among qualifiers in at least one common admissible state; rows that can never be cheapest are excluded. Published per bar: the confirmed unique cheapest (if any), the certified members of K_r, unresolved memberships, and provisional status beside each candidate. Fixtures: a singleton, confirmed-qualified, non-provisional row → confirmed cheapest; a provisional singleton → no confirmed-cheapest label (it may be in K_r) and, qualifying in every scenario, a published 100% event frequency but no scenario-supported label; a cheaper provisional qualifier still prevents a non-provisional candidate's unique label; a uniquely cheapest candidate; two equal-cost qualifiers → both in K_r, no unique label. The reference configuration always qualifies against itself. **Claim contract (E51)**: every interval result publishes decision, target (the published record, O25), universe, evidence-set coverage (structural — exact bounds on the published record — or "joint coverage unspecified"), solver status, selected-cohort extension and its witness or certificate; displayed wording is "confirmed within the declared evidence set", never a confidence statement. **Scope (E38)**: every frontier and bar result publishes its universe ("among these n evaluable configurations") and whether it **extends to the selected cohort** — established only when every row outside R_W is ruled out by structural bounds (e.g. full-domain quality bounds below the bar, or a cost lower bound above the candidate's cost upper bound); otherwise "not established", with the omitted competitors listed.
