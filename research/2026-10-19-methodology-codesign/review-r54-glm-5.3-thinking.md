Against the stated, bounded objective—and respecting the binding owner decisions—I found no remaining material methodological defect requiring a new formula, weight, eligibility rule, normalization scheme or uncertainty treatment.

The important distinction is between a defensible methodology and strong empirical conclusions. A release can correctly produce exploratory Plan scores, unavailable comparisons or unresolved results when public evidence does not identify the relevant quantity. Those outcomes do not themselves establish a defect in the methodology.

I also would not add another sensitivity method merely to broaden the checklist. Uncertainty and sensitivity analysis should make the consequences of constructing a composite indicator more transparent; their presence is not, by itself, evidence that its ordering is reliable. That distinction is consistent with the composite-indicator literature and is the appropriate standard for assessing the draft’s existing machinery.
