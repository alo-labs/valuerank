H1. Plan admission decision table (E106) omits the normative case for target-workload identified measurements

Issue: E106 introduces three defining predicates (OfficialDisplayEligible, TargetTransportIdentified, PlanIntervalEligible) and states that every route is classified once into one of its cases before comparator evaluation. However, the five listed cases only define execution-identity incompatibility (Case 1), owner assumptions (Case 2), measurements whose target-workload transport is unidentified (Case 3), valuation-basis mismatches (Case 4), and evidence rule failures (Case 5). There is no explicit case for a controlled measurement or report partition where target-workload transport is identified (TargetTransportIdentified = True and PlanIntervalEligible = True under FullCost).

Why it hurts the objective: E106 specifies that "other sections refer to this table rather than restating admission (fixtures: one per case)." Any build implementation relying on E106 as an exhaustive decision table has no matching branch for an identified measurement. When category-mix bounds (E210) or compatible workload measurements establish set or point identification, such routes fall through or risk misclassification under Case 3 as interval-ineligible.

Concrete recommendation: Split Case 3 or add an explicit Case 3a:

Case 3a: Exact-route or O88 measurement with target-workload transport identified (point- or set-identified under E67) $\rightarrow$ OfficialDisplayEligible = True, TargetTransportIdentified = True, and PlanIntervalEligible = True (conditional on FullCost and valuation-gate passage); displayed multiple is $M_W$ (point) or $L_W$ (set-identified lower bound).

Case 3b: Measurement whose target-workload transport is unidentified $\rightarrow$ OfficialDisplayEligible = True, TargetTransportIdentified = False, PlanIntervalEligible = False; displayed multiple is $M_{\text{observed}}$ (or O88 figure), scored and flagged "workload-unmatched" (O16).

H2. Contradiction between E151 (Part 4) and O127/Part 5 regarding earlier-admitted report lifecycles

Issue: Part 4 under E151 states: "The age stages below govern controlled measurements, transfers, and earlier-admitted reports that stay published as diagnostics after leaving the window." In direct contrast, Part 5 states: "A report not yet admitted counts only inside the current window; a report admitted at an earlier release (admittedOn) keeps counting outside it and ages on its own clock like a measurement (owner decision O127)." Decision ledger O127 explicitly affirms that an admitted report pair keeps counting after the 30-day window and ages like a measurement.

Why it hurts the objective: Part 4 retains superseded pre-O127 language implying that admitted reports immediately lapse to diagnostic upon exiting the 30-day window $[V - 30\text{ d}, V]$. This causes ambiguity in evidence-stage clock evaluation and partition rebuilding between E151 and E212.

Concrete recommendation: In Part 4 under E151, amend the sentence to align with O127: "The age stages below govern controlled measurements, transfers, and earlier-admitted reports (which keep counting outside the 30-day window and age on their own clocks like measurements under O127)."

M1. E88 dominance pre-filter and E116 canonical ID omit allocation regime matching

Issue: E88 filters a route candidate $b$ when another admissible candidate $a$ has an evidence grade at least $b$'s and $L_a > U_b$ for the same (configuration, plan, workload, valuation basis). In addition, E116 defines the canonical measurement-object ID from (route ID, partition and report IDs, measurement dates, workload, valuation basis, identification-object version). Neither includes allocation regime (E220).

Why it hurts the objective: E220 mandates that SemiAnalysis results preserve their allocation regime (e.g., Opus 5.5's all-Opus allocation vs. Fable's 50% shared pool), requiring that "measurements under different regimes are never compared or pooled as one." Under E88, a measurement under an unshared regime could dominate and remove an identified measurement under a shared-pool regime for the same configuration and plan. Furthermore, if two measurement objects for the same route differ solely by allocation regime, their canonical IDs under E116 collide.

Concrete recommendation:

Add allocation regime to E88's pre-filter matching tuple: candidates must match on (configuration, plan, workload, valuation basis, allocation regime) before interval dominance is evaluated.

Add allocation regime to E116's canonical measurement-object ID hash inputs.

M2. Underspecification of the representative plan row selection rule in E245 (Part 6)

Issue: O48 and E245 establish that the Plan ranking has one row per configuration and plan, and E88/E85 comparator runs per plan row ("routes on different plans never filter each other"). Part 6 then states: "A configuration's own Plan figures (the README table, model pages, and the Plan diagnostics that rebuild anchors or move evidence) are those of its representative row, the plan row of its selected route; the README and site list every plan row." The text does not formally specify how the "selected route" across different plans is chosen for the representative row.

Why it hurts the objective: In v2, the comparator operated globally across all plans using key 3 (highest eligible Value Multiple). In v3, because E85 is defined per plan row, selecting a single representative plan row across multiple plan tiers (e.g., ChatGPT Plus vs. Pro 100 vs. Pro 200 vs. Pro 500) requires a documented cross-plan tie-breaker. While Part 6 notes that "GPT-6 Astra (max)'s selected route moved from ChatGPT Plus to a higher plan under O45", the rule governing cross-plan choice for representative rows is implicit.

Concrete recommendation: Explicitly state in E245 that a configuration's representative plan row is determined by running the E85 lexicographic comparator across the configuration's plan-level winning routes, where comparator key 3 selects the plan row with the largest comparison multiple (or lowest effective cash cost).

L1. Loose phrasing regarding calibration availability in E249 vs. E164

Issue: E164 states that measured-evidence calibration requires at least two reference rows ("measured-evidence calibration unavailable" below two references). E249 summarizes this as: "which says when the calibration is unavailable because no row has a measured route."

Why it hurts the objective: If exactly one plan row has a measured route, E249's phrasing implies calibration might attempt to run, whereas E164's condition correctly catches that at least two reference points are mathematically required to construct $c_L = \text{cheapest}/2$ and $c_H = \text{dearest} \times 2$.
