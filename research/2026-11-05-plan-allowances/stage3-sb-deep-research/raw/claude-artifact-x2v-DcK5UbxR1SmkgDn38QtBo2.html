How much Opus 5.5 a Pro plan buys

We ran 147 controlled Opus 5.5 calls and watched the plan's own usage meter. A full 5-hour window works out to about $26–29 at API prices for output-heavy work, and about $44 for cache-heavy agentic coding. A week holds about 7 such windows.

The agentic mix is this account's own usage earlier in the week: 99% cache reads, 0.2% output. Thinking tokens count as output. The weekly cap is the one that binds: a week contains about 33 five-hour windows, but only about 7 fit under it. The monthly figure assumes the weekly cap is hit every week. At a $20/month Pro price, that is roughly 40–70× the subscription in API terms.

Method

  1. Starting point. The plan meter read Pro, 5-hour window freshly reset at 0%, weekly at 4%.
  2. Load. Headless claude -p --model claude-opus-5-5 --effort medium calls with no tools and no MCP servers. Every call's token usage was logged.
  3. Price check. Each call's reported cost matched list prices exactly: $4/M input, $20/M output, $8/M for 1-hour cache writes, $0.20/M cache reads.
  4. Ledger. All Opus 5.5 usage on this machine since the window reset was summed from local transcripts, counting each message once. That includes the controlling session's own turns.
  5. Two batch types. Batches A1 and A2 were output-heavy (20 essays each, about 5,000 output tokens per call). Batches B0–B2 were cache-read-heavy (86 calls over a fixed 106k-token context, with one-sentence answers).
  6. Fit. The meter was read after each batch and fitted to a weighted cost model.
meter % = (output $ + a · cache-write $ + b · cache-read $) / L × 100

Cached tokens count at about half price

Output-heavy batches alone give $27.5–28.6 per full window. Pricing everything at list overshoots once the cache-heavy batches start. The best fit counts cache writes at about 0.55× and cache reads at about 0.5× list, with L ≈ $25.75 of output-equivalent per 5 hours. Its squared error is about 10× lower than the list-price model's.

Meter readingFitted weights (0.55 / 0.5, L = $25.75)All tokens at list price (L = $31.75)

Weekly ratio. The weekly meter moved 3 points while the 5-hour meter moved 28. The earlier 4% is explained by this week's prior usage, which comes to about 23% of a 5-hour window under the fitted weights. Taken together, one week ≈ 7.2 five-hour windows (range 6.8–7.8).

Limits of this measurement

  • The meter shows whole percents only, so each reading is ±0.5 point. The weekly ratio rests on a few points of movement.
  • The cache weights come from a three-parameter fit to five readings. The real rule could be compute-based rather than tied to price.
  • Usage from claude.ai or other devices is not in the local logs. If there was any, the true caps are larger.
  • One time of day, one day. Limits may vary with load.
  • Batch B2 took about 20 minutes instead of about 3. It may have been throttled; this was not checked.
  • The run itself used 28% of the account's 5-hour window and about 3 points of its weekly limit.
