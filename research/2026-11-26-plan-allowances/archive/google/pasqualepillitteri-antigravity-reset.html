Google gives all Antigravity users a free quota reset for its first birthday

Google has reset Antigravity's usage quota for every user and every model to mark the platform's first birthday, Varun Mohan announced on X on November 18.

Google has zeroed out the Antigravity usage quota for every user and across all models to celebrate the platform's first year. Varun Mohan, a director at Google DeepMind who works on the project, announced it in an X post at 12:38 a.m. Italian time on November 18, 2026 (November 17 in the United States). Antigravity launched on November 18, 2025.

To work out whether the subscription pays off, our Google AI Pro guide explains what it includes, how it differs from Plus, Ultra and free Gemini, and how to calculate the value.

Antigravity is the platform Google uses to have AI agents write code. The agents write, run and test code with varying degrees of autonomy, from a desktop app, from an editor (the IDE), from the command line or through an SDK (the kit for building your own agents), as Wikipedia lays out. It is free to use, with a quota that renews every week, or through the Google AI Pro and Ultra subscriptions, which come with larger quotas. It debuted on November 18, 2025, the same day as Gemini 3, as a free preview.

Anyone developing on Windows can also check what Windows 11 telemetry collects about coding agents.

Try it now

Antigravity can be downloaded from antigravity.google for Windows, macOS and Linux, and it works with a personal Google account. The free plan has a weekly quota, while Pro and Ultra get a wider quota that renews every five hours.

Open Antigravity Plans and quotas

What Mohan wrote

The post runs two lines. After a "One more thing…", Mohan says that for Antigravity's first birthday the quota is being reset for everyone, across all models. There is no start or end time, no distinction between plans and no figure. The replies are mostly birthday wishes, plus one practical question that comes up in several messages, namely how much headroom people who were close to the ceiling actually get back.

One more thing…

In honor of Antigravity turning 1, we’re resetting quota for everyone across all models!

— Varun Mohan (@_mohansolo) November 17, 2026

A reset gives back the quota already consumed, and nothing more. The ceiling that applies once that quota runs out again stays what it was, as RuntimeWire also notes among the outlets that picked up the post.

What changes across the three plans

The plans documentation distinguishes three tiers. Anyone without a Google AI subscription gets a quota that renews once a week. Google AI Pro gives a wider quota, renewed every five hours until the weekly ceiling is reached. Google AI Ultra has the highest quota, again in five-hour windows, plus access to third-party models. Subscribers can also buy AI credits to work beyond the base quota. Quotas, for that matter, are not all equal even within a single plan: back in October Gemini 4 Argon landed on Antigravity with a separate quota for AI Pro users.

For the free plan, the reset is worth a full week of work. For a Pro subscriber stuck against the weekly ceiling it is worth even more, since until now the only option was to wait for the renewal. The documentation specifies that limits follow the work the agent performs. Complex tasks burn more quota than a simple request. With several agents running in parallel (as in the guide to /swarm) the same stretch of time holds more work, and the quota drops faster.

A year of quotas, from launch to today

From day one, quota has been Antigravity's weak spot. At launch Google offered a free preview with generous limits on the Gemini 3 Pro models, renewed every five hours. The next day, in his review for DevClass, Tim Anderson reported that one user had burned through the free quota in about twenty minutes, with no way to buy more. His own test sessions, he wrote, had also failed because the model providers were overloaded (the account comes from Wikipedia).

In December 2025 Google raised the limits for Pro and Ultra subscribers and moved free users to a weekly ceiling, the setup that still holds. With Antigravity 2.0, which arrived in May 2026 together with the command line and the SDK, a $100-a-month Ultra plan appeared with five times the Pro limits, while the high-end Ultra dropped from $250 to $200 a month.

Every step, from the five-hour windows to the weekly ceiling, from quotas calculated on compute to the new Ultra plans, has changed how much work an agent can do before it stops. On the Google developer forum, threads from Pro subscribers describing lockouts of days instead of the promised window are still open, such as the one titled ten-day lockout instead of the five-hour reset. These are reports from individual users, not Google data, but they explain why a reset, even a birthday one, makes news among people who use the platform every day.

Not the first reset of the year

After Google I/O in May, Gemini received usage limits calculated on compute; in Antigravity some people burned through them in an hour of work, 9to5Google reports. On Wednesday, May 20, Google tripled the limits of the Gemini models in Antigravity and zeroed out everyone's weekly quotas. The following evening it tripled them again, this time on the weekly ceiling, resetting the paid plans once more. Mohan admitted that a couple of work sessions were enough to hit the weekly limit.

In early June everyone's counters were zeroed, free and paid alike, together with a corrected version of Gemini 3.5 Flash after complaints about the quality of the low-effort variant, Ubergizmo recounts.

This time there is nothing to fix. It is a birthday present, met with good wishes, that leaves the May sore point where it was, namely limits lower than before, as 9to5Google pointed out. Nor is it Google's only gesture this autumn: in October it had already offered Antigravity free for six months to 10,000 scientists. A year after its debut the platform has more models than it did then, among them Gemini 4 Hydrogen (available to everyone in mid-November) and Gemini 4 Carbon, which costs double from November 30. The problem every agent platform faces remains, the ceiling.

How to check your quota and what to do with credits

Antigravity's settings page shows base quota usage across the various models, the documentation says. It is the first place to look after the reset, since the post does not say how much each plan's headroom grows. Write down the quota percentage and the date of the next renewal before and after the reset. It is the only way to notice whether the weekly window has shifted, as the user quoted in the box below reports.

Anyone who has bought AI credits will find the AI Credit Overages option in the settings. With "Never", Antigravity waits for the quota to renew before using that model again. With "Always" it switches to credits as soon as the base quota runs out and goes back to the quota when it renews. Because credits only kick in once the quota is exhausted, after the reset the "Always" option stops consuming them until the next time it runs out.

Anyone with long jobs waiting would do well to run them now, while the counter is full. For those starting from scratch there is the complete guide to using Antigravity.

Limitations to keep in mind

  • The announcement is all in a two-line post: no time, no distinction between the five-hour window and the weekly ceiling, not a word on AI credits. Anyone near the limit finds out the regained headroom only by opening the settings.
  • The reset does not raise plan ceilings. It gives back quota already used, while the discontent in May was precisely about limits lower than before. That problem remains open.
  • The weekly window may shift. One user, among the replies to the post, writes that they previously had 95% of quota and three days to renewal, and now have 100% and six days. It is the report of a single person, not a confirmation from Google, but if it held for everyone, those close to their natural renewal would gain little or nothing.

Frequently asked questions

1. Does the reset also apply to people using Antigravity for free?

The post says "everyone", without distinguishing between plans. The free quota renews every week, so those who had used it up find it full again. How much extra headroom each plan gets, however, Mohan does not specify.

2. Are purchased AI credits affected?

The post only talks about quota. According to the documentation, credits are a separate item, consumed beyond the base quota and dependent on the AI Credit Overages option set to "Never" or "Always".

3. Does the reset also cover the Anthropic and OpenAI models inside Antigravity?

Mohan writes "all models". In the selector there are, besides the Gemini models, Claude Opus and Sonnet (the 5.5 versions only for Pro and Ultra subscribers) and GPT-OSS-120B, according to Wikipedia. Which of these actually fall under the reset depends on each person's plan, and Google has not explained it.
