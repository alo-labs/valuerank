ChatGPT and Codex will share usage limits for paid users starting in December

Using one AI tool could limit how much you can use another.

TL;DR
  • Starting in December, ChatGPT, Work, and Codex will share a single usage allowance across Plus, Pro, and Business plans.
  • Usage will be measured in tokens rather than messages, with more advanced models and complex tasks consuming more of your allowance.
  • Instant chat will be exempt from the shared allowance, but OpenAI hasn’t disclosed how large the new limits will be.

ChatGPT has grown into much more than a chatbot, with OpenAI introducing tools like Work and Codex to help users tackle everything from building presentations to developing apps. Until now, you could use these tools without worrying about how they would affect your regular ChatGPT allowance. But as OpenAI brings Chat and Work together, it’s also changing how much you can do with your paid subscription.

OpenAI’s Thibault Sottiaux announced on X that the company has begun rolling out the combined Chat and Work experience to a small group of Pro users ahead of a wider release. However, a new support page reveals the catch that didn’t make it into the announcement. Starting in December, ChatGPT and Codex will share a usage allowance across Plus, Pro, and Business plans.

Stephen Radochia / Android Authority

Currently, Work and Codex already share usage limits, while regular ChatGPT conversations have their own. You could exhaust your Codex allowance building an app and still switch to ChatGPT’s Thinking models to discuss ideas or work through complex questions. Come December, those activities will draw from the same pot.

OpenAI is also changing how it measures usage. Rather than counting messages, the company will measure consumption in tokens, the small chunks of information AI models read and generate. Asking ChatGPT a relatively simple question using a Thinking model could consume considerably less of your allowance than asking it to analyze a lengthy document.

This means spending hours coding with Codex could leave you with less room to use ChatGPT for other demanding tasks, such as deep research. Similarly, heavy ChatGPT usage could limit how much coding you can get done with Codex. The good news, however, is that Instant chat won’t count toward the shared allowance. You’ll still be able to use ChatGPT for everyday conversations without eating into your budget for more demanding tasks.

OpenAI expects most users won’t hit their limits as often as they do today, but it hasn’t revealed how large the combined allowances will be. You’ll be able to track your remaining usage under Settings > Usage and purchase credits for eligible features, upgrade your plan, or wait for a reset when you run out.

Some users may see these changes before December as OpenAI gathers feedback.
