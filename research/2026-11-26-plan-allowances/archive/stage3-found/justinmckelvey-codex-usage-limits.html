Quick Answer

Codex usage limits are a rolling five-hour allowance per model, with a weekly cap that may sit on top, and OpenAI publishes ranges rather than numbers. As of November 23, 2026 its own page estimates a Plus ($20/month) user gets roughly 17–180 GPT-6.2 Sol messages per five-hour window (6–50 on GPT-6.2 Astra, 350–3,000 on GPT-6 Luna), and Pro ($100, $200 or $500) currently has no five-hour limit at all. TL;DR: Codex Usage Limits Are a Window, Not a Count

The question "what are the Codex usage limits" has no single-number answer, and that is by design. OpenAI's pricing page, read today, describes an allowance that refills on a rolling five-hour window, varies by which model you run, may also be capped weekly, and is spent by tokens rather than by messages. Plus is the base allowance and a Standard Business seat matches it; a Premium Business seat gets more included usage with no 5-hour limit, and Pro ($100, $200 or $500) currently has no five-hour limit either. Nothing here is a fixed cap, which is why two people on the same plan report wildly different experiences. One runs Luna for quick edits and never sees the limit; the other keeps a 40-file repo in context on Sol and hits it before lunch.

I run Codex, Claude Code, and Cursor side by side on real client work, and the honest version of this post is that the limits stopped being the interesting question once I understood the meter. Here is the meter.

What changed in November 2026

  • Two new models with their own rows. GPT-6.2 Astra (6–50 per five hours on Plus) and GPT-6.2 Sol (17–180) now top OpenAI's table. Both give a little more per window than the GPT-6 and 6.1 versions they sit above.
  • Pro got more, not less. OpenAI's Pro article now says Pro 100 and Pro 500 get twice their previous Work and Codex allowance, and the lower Pro 200 allowance announced on September 29 is canceled. Every Pro 200 subscriber, new or old, is on the same, higher allowance at $200.
  • ChatGPT and Codex share one meter from December. On Plus, Pro and Business, ChatGPT and Codex will draw from one allowance measured in tokens. Instant chat won't count against it.
  • Premium Business seats lost their "5x". OpenAI's Business article no longer says Premium is five times Standard. It says Premium has higher included usage and no five-hour limit.

December 2026: Codex and ChatGPT share one allowance

Starting in December 2026, on Plus, Pro and Business, Codex stops having its own separate budget: it shares one token-based allowance with ChatGPT. Source: OpenAI's help article Understanding ChatGPT and Codex usage limits (read November 9, 2026).

  • How it's measured: tokens, not messages. The model you pick and the size of the task decide how fast the allowance goes.
  • What's left out: Instant chat in ChatGPT doesn't draw from it.
  • Where to check: Settings, then Usage in ChatGPT for the combined number. Inside a Codex CLI session, /status still shows what you have left.
  • At the limit: wait for the reset, upgrade, or buy credits.

The practical change: a long ChatGPT work session in the afternoon leaves less for Codex that evening, and the other way around. The ranges below (15 to 160 GPT-6.1 Sol messages on Plus, per chatgpt.com/codex/pricing) describe today's setup.

One Astra message, in other models' messages

OpenAI never says how the models compare, but its own ranges do. Divide each model's range by GPT-6 Astra's on the same plan and you get an exchange rate for the window. The ratios hold for a standard Business seat too, because its column is identical to Plus.

Model Plus range Messages worth one Astra message (small tasks / large tasks)
GPT-6 Astra 5–45 1 / 1
GPT-6.2 Astra 6–50 1.1 / 1.2
GPT-6.2 Sol 17–180 4 / 3.4
GPT-6 Sol 15–150 3.3 / 3
GPT-6.1 Sol 15–160 3.6 / 3
GPT-6 Luna 350–3,000 67 / 70

A worked morning, to make that concrete. Say you send ten Codex turns on Plus. On Astra, ten turns are 22% of a window if your tasks are small enough to sit at the top of OpenAI's range (45 per window), and two full windows if they're big enough to sit at the bottom (5 per window), which is ten hours of waiting. The same ten turns on GPT-6 Sol are 7% to 67% of one window. The assumption doing the work: a task that sits at the top of one model's range sits at the top of the others' too. OpenAI doesn't state that, but it is the only way to read ranges published side by side.

The practical rule it gives you: each Astra turn costs about three to four Sol turns, so keep it for the turn that needs it and let GPT-6.2 Sol or Luna do the rest. GPT-6.2 Sol is now the best value of the strong models on the table: the most messages per window of any Sol.

Codex on Free and Go, and the "monthly limit" question

Free ($0) and Go ($8 a month) are both listed as "GPT-6 Luna at Standard speed in the desktop app, subject to rollout." Neither gets a column in the estimate table, so OpenAI publishes no per-window number for either. OpenAI's Codex help article confirms Codex is included on Free and Go, but Codex Cloud isn't; the web, CLI, IDE and iOS surfaces and the cloud integrations are listed on the Plus card. Image generation isn't available on Free at all.

There is no monthly Codex limit on a ChatGPT plan. The two clocks are the five-hour window and the weekly cap. The only monthly-shaped meters are on the business side: Enterprise and Edu workspaces with flexible pricing have "no fixed rate limits" and spend credits instead, and without flexible pricing they get "the same per-seat usage limits as Plus."

How to check your Codex usage (and what uses it up)

There are two places to look, and OpenAI names both on its Codex pricing page (read October 10, 2026). The usage dashboard at chatgpt.com/codex/settings/usage shows your current limits and when each one resets. The /status command shows what's left from inside an active Codex CLI session, which is the one to run before you start a long task, not after it stalls.

What you'll see is a share of an allowance, not a message count, because OpenAI's numbers are estimates: 15 to 160 GPT-6.1 Sol local messages per five hours on Plus and on a Standard Business seat, and no five-hour limit on Pro plans. Where you land in that range depends on what you ask for. Three things move it most:

  • Speed mode. Fast uses your included usage at 2.5x the Standard rate. Ultrafast on GPT-6 Astra or GPT-6.1 Sol uses it at 8x.
  • Image generation. 3 to 5x faster than a similar turn without images, depending on quality and size.
  • Cloud tasks and big context. Cloud tasks may use more than local messages, and long sessions that hold a lot of the codebase in context cost more per message than small scripts.

OpenAI's own advice is to check the dashboard every week or two to learn your pace. Mine is the same with a deadline: three normal working days, then decide whether you need more room or a smaller model.

The 5-hour window: what resets and when

The window is rolling, not calendar-based. Usage you spent at 9am frees up at 2pm regardless of what you did in between, which is why a heavy morning and a heavy afternoon feel like two separate budgets and a heavy single stretch feels like a wall. The ChatGPT usage dashboard shows current limits and reset times; inside a Codex CLI session, /status shows the same thing without leaving the terminal. If you have never typed that command, type it before you decide you need a bigger plan.

The Codex weekly limit

OpenAI states that a weekly cap may apply on top of the five-hour allowance and does not publish it, which is the single most-searched frustration about this product and the reason "codex weekly limit" is its own query. Read the two meters together and the order of events is predictable: on an ordinary week the five-hour window is the one you meet, and the weekly cap only shows itself after several days of long agentic runs in a row, at which point the five-hour reset stops helping. If you are hitting it, you are a heavy user by any definition, and the plan question below is a real one for you rather than a hypothetical.

Codex rate limits vs usage limits

The two phrases get used for the same thing, and the page itself mixes them. The rolling window and the weekly cap are what a ChatGPT-plan user means by either. The other meaning, rate limits in the API sense (requests and tokens per minute by API tier), only applies if you authenticate Codex with an API key instead of a ChatGPT login. That route drops the plan windows entirely, bills at API pricing, and gives up the cloud features (GitHub code review, Slack) that live on the ChatGPT side. It is the right choice for CI and shared environments and the wrong one for a person who wants a predictable monthly bill.

If you do switch Codex to an API key, you stop paying in windows and start paying per token, so look up the model you run in OpenAI API pricing before you move a long session over. Cached input bills at 10% of the input price, which helps a long session that keeps resending the same context.

What burns the allowance faster than you think

  • Context. A session holding a large codebase costs more per turn than a fresh one; the page lists context, reasoning, tool use, retrieval, and caching as the variables, and says prompt length alone is not a reliable estimate.
  • Cloud chats. OpenAI says cloud tasks may use more of the allowance than local messages.
  • Fast mode. On GPT-6.2 Astra and GPT-6.2 Sol, Fast uses included usage at 2x the Standard rate and Ultrafast at 6x (purchased credits pay 3x for Ultrafast through February 28, 2027, then 6x). On the older models, Fast is 2.5x (2x on credits) and Ultrafast is 8x (6x on credits) on GPT-6 Astra and GPT-6.1 Sol. OpenAI notes these are allowance rates, not speed guarantees. Among the Pro plans, Pro 500 includes Ultrafast and Pro 200 gets it for GPT-6.2 Sol in Codex only.
  • MCP servers and a big AGENTS.md. OpenAI's own tips: every connected MCP server adds context to every message, so disconnect the ones you aren't using, and nest AGENTS.md files instead of keeping one giant one at the root.
  • Images. Image generation draws on the same limits roughly three to five times faster on average.
  • Voice. Voice in Desktop now spends your existing Codex budget "at $0.05 per minute," and the model doing the task is billed on top at its normal rate. On credit-billed workspaces it's 1.25 credits a minute.

What happens when you hit the limit mid-task

The page is explicit that work in progress gets to finish: if you reach the limit during an active turn, the agent continues that turn subject to fair use. So a long migration does not die halfway; it stops accepting the next instruction. From there you have four moves, cheapest first: wait for the window, switch to a smaller model (Terra or Luna instead of Sol) so what remains lasts longer, buy additional credits (Plus and Pro can do this without changing plan), or run extra local chats against an API key at usage rates. Upgrading is the fourth option, and it is the one people reach for first.

What is Luna Reserve (the "GPT Reserve" limit) in Codex?

If your usage screen shows a second meter called Luna Reserve, or a model labeled gpt-reserve, that is OpenAI's fallback allowance, not a new plan. OpenAI's help article on it (re-read November 23, 2026) says it gives selected accounts extra usage on GPT-5.6 Luna once regular usage runs out. Four details matter more than the name:

  • Personal Plus and Pro accounts only. Business and Enterprise workspaces don't get it. So the same developer can "last longer" on a home Plus account than on the company's Business seat, and the plan isn't the reason.
  • It doesn't refill your regular allowance. The five-hour window and the weekly limit above keep their own clocks. The reserve is a separate allowance with its own meter, and OpenAI doesn't publish its size.
  • It doesn't unlock the stronger models. You get Luna, the small model. By the exchange rates above, a Luna turn is the cheapest turn in the window. That's also why it can't stand in for Sol or Astra on a hard refactor.
  • Eligibility can change. "Selected accounts" means you might not have it at all, and having it this week is no promise for next week.

What I'd do with it: let the reserve handle the cleanup (renames, tests, small edits) after the window runs out, and save the big task for the reset. If your team runs on Business seats and keeps hitting the wall, the reserve won't help you. The options are the four moves in the section above.

Should you upgrade to Pro for the limits?

Decide it from the meter, not from a bad afternoon. OpenAI's page itself suggests checking the usage dashboard "every week or two" to learn your pace. Run /status at the end of three normal working days and note how often you were actually blocked versus annoyed. If Plus blocks you most days, Pro at $100 is the honest tier, and it is worth knowing that OpenAI now sells that middle rung; the "$20 then $200 cliff" I described in my Codex pricing breakdown has had a $100 step in it since September 2026, Pro drops the five-hour window entirely, and as of November Pro 100 carries twice its previous Work and Codex allowance. If you are blocked once a week, buy credits and keep the $20 plan. If you are blocked because every session starts by re-explaining the whole codebase, fix the AGENTS.md and the task scoping first; how to use Codex covers both, and it is free. The same three-step order (threads, scoping, then money) is what I wrote for the Claude side in Claude usage limits. If you're weighing a switch rather than an upgrade, Claude's, Gemini's and Copilot's ceilings sit next to OpenAI's in the AI usage limits by plan table.

Codex rate limits: what resets and when

"Codex rate limits" is the phrase people type. The thing they are hitting is the plan allowance above, so here is the reset behavior in one place, from OpenAI's Codex pricing page and its Codex help articles, re-read on November 23, 2026. There are two clocks. The five-hour window refills on its own schedule, and the usage dashboard shows the exact reset time. The weekly limit resets on a fixed date; OpenAI's help center confirms both exist by describing how a banked reset "refreshes your 5-hour and weekly Codex usage windows and changes your weekly reset date." OpenAI publishes neither the weekly number nor a reset day.

You can also buy a reset, per OpenAI's help article (re-read November 23, 2026). Personal Plus and Pro accounts can buy an instant reset from Usage settings in the desktop app, or from a banner once they hit the weekly limit. Hitting only the five-hour limit doesn't trigger the banner. It isn't available on Free, Go, Business, Enterprise or Edu. Two things to know before you pay. It applies the moment checkout finishes, even if you have usage left, and it can't be saved for later. And it pulls your normal weekly allowance forward rather than adding extra usage: your next automatic weekly reset lands 7 days after your first request following the reset, so your weekly date moves. OpenAI shows the price at checkout, publishes no price in the article, and says it generally doesn't refund these.

What hitting one looks like: OpenAI publishes no exact error string. In the app you get a limit banner that names which allowance is exhausted and lists your options: add credits, apply an available reset, upgrade, or wait for the displayed reset time. In the CLI, /status shows token usage and /usage shows daily, weekly, or cumulative activity and lets you redeem an earned reset. Support will not reset a limit for you; the help center says so directly.

Two things that are not this limit. API rate limits (requests and tokens per minute by tier) belong to an API key and only apply if you signed in with one. And ChatGPT's own caps on images, uploads, and voice have separate banners and, in OpenAI's words, "do not apply to Codex." One thing that doesn't spend the limit at all: auto-review safety checks, when you're signed in with a ChatGPT account, are free and don't count toward your plan's usage.

How many Codex messages do you get on ChatGPT Plus?
OpenAI's Codex pricing page, re-read November 9, 2026, estimates a Plus ($20 a month) user gets 15 to 160 GPT-6.1 Sol local messages per five-hour window, 35 to 285 on GPT-6.1 Terra, 15 to 150 on GPT-6 Sol, 5 to 45 on GPT-6 Astra and 350 to 3,000 on GPT-6 Luna. Those are estimates, not fixed limits. Local messages and cloud chats share the same allowance, a weekly limit may also apply, and a Standard Business seat gets the same ranges. When it runs out you can wait for the window, switch to a smaller model, or buy ChatGPT credits.
Does Codex share a usage limit with ChatGPT?
From December 2026 it does, on Plus, Pro and Business. OpenAI says the allowance will be shared across ChatGPT and Codex and measured in tokens, so heavy ChatGPT work leaves less for Codex. Instant chat is excluded. Settings, then Usage shows the combined total; /status in the Codex CLI shows what's left in a session.
