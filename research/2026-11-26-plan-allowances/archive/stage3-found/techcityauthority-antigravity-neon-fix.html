Users say Antigravity still burns weekly quotas after Google's Neon fix

TCA Staff November 23, 2026

Image credit: Google Antigravity, official artwork from the company's Antigravity 2.0 blog post.

Paying Google AI Pro users say Gemini 4 Neon in Google's Antigravity coding tool is still eating through weekly quotas, even after a cache change and a quota reset on November 21. The complaints continued into the morning of November 23, at the time of this writing. This follows earlier TechcityAuthority reporting that Google is locking paying Antigravity users out over terms of service, and that Gemini 4 Argon users said limits run out after a few prompts.

Early on November 21, Varun Mohan, who leads the Antigravity team at Google DeepMind, said Google was rolling out a change to increase the Neon cache hit rate on Antigravity by around 4% by making the cache pool much bigger. He said that would increase what users can get done in quota by at least 20%, and that Google would issue a reset afterward because the old behavior had burned quota faster. In a follow-up post a few minutes later, he said the hit rate was particularly bad for workloads with more time between model invocations, because the cache was evicted. He wrote that this should fix that, that those users should see a much higher improvement, and that Google will continue to improve this metric globally, adding, "sorry for the inconvenience."

That afternoon, Mohan posted that Google had reset everyone's Gemini quota on Antigravity, and that the caching improvements had increased the amount users can get done in quota by 22%.

Reset everyone's Gemini quota on Antigravity.

The caching improvements have increased the amount you can get done in quota by 22%. Enjoy building with Neon over the weekend!

— Varun Mohan (@_mohansolo) November 21, 2026

Users say the weekly cap is still running down quickly. Arun, posting as hiarun02, said one 5-hour session with Gemini 4 Neon and Hydrogen "reportedly burned almost 50% of the entire weekly limit."

google AI Pro limits are getting ridiculous

one 5-hour session with Gemini 4 Neon + Hydrogen reportedly burned almost 50% of the entire weekly limit.

imagine paying for a monthly plan and losing half your weekly usage in just 5 hours.

what's even the point of a 5-hour reset anymore?

— Arun (@hiarun02) November 23, 2026

Tekon Burns, posting as TekonBurns, cited local logs from two developers as user analysis, not a Google figure. He said that across 385 Neon High calls, zero-cache misses rose from 10% at 50k to 100k tokens to 65% at 300k to 500k tokens, and that this used a full 5-hour bar plus 58% of the weekly quota in 3 hours.

Antigravity users running long Gemini 4 Neon High sessions: watch your context length.

Google shipped its caching patch and weekend reset promising 22% more quota, yet two developers pulled fresh local DB logs today.

Across 385 Neon High calls, cache misses exploded as context grew:

50k to 100k tokens: 10% zero-cache misses
250k to 300k tokens: 43% misses
300k to 500k tokens: 65% misses

That wiped an entire 5-hour bar plus 58% of weekly quota in 3 hours.

Consider using /compact regularly!

— Tekon Burns (@TekonBurns) November 23, 2026

CrossstarDesigns, posting as crossstardesign, said their Google AI Pro weekly quota was burning out in a single day of coding. Yash Verma, posting as y4sh97, said they paid for Google AI Pro and that two tasks later, 90% of the weekly limit was gone, with Neon as orchestrator and Hydrogen as executor, and that the weekly cap would reset in 5 days.

A related report in the Antigravity CLI GitHub repository, issue 1665, remained open at the time of this writing and is labelled as a billing and quota issue. A newer open follow-up, issue 1720, and a thread on the Google AI Developers Forum, describe cache misses on unchanged prefixes still burning quota after the November 21 change, with measurements dated November 22. None of those threads had a reply from Google staff at the time of this writing.

At the time of this writing, Mohan, the official Antigravity account, Google AI Studio, and Logan Kilpatrick had not posted a further quota reset or a new cache fix addressing the reports that came in after the November 21 reset. Mohan later said the team was still trying to improve the long tail, and that workloads with more time between model invocations should see a much bigger win. He also kept posting about other Antigravity work, including harness results for Neon, but those posts did not address the later weekly-quota complaints.
