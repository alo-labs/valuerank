Go

OpenCode Go gives you reliable access to popular open coding models, with three monthly plans:

PlanPriceIncluded usage
Go$10/monthLower-cost access to the models below
Go Plus$40/monthHigher usage limits across the models below
Go Teams$20/member/month, 2-member minimumEvery member gets their own limits, $5/month higher than Go on every model

Go works like any other provider in OpenCode. You subscribe to OpenCode Go and get your API key. It’s completely optional and you don’t need it to use OpenCode.

The service is designed primarily for international users and provides stable global access.

  1. Sign in to the OpenCode Console, subscribe to Go, Go Plus, or Go Teams, add your billing details, and copy your API key.

  2. Run /connect in the TUI, select OpenCode Go, and paste your API key.

    /connect

  3. Run /models to select a model available through Go.

    /models
Note

Go and Go Plus cover the member who subscribes and the workspace’s API keys. Other members you invite use free models or the workspace balance. Either can upgrade to Go Teams at any time, with the unused part of the month’s allowance credited. Go Teams covers every member of the workspace at $20 per member per month, with a 2-member minimum ($40/month). The second seat covers the next member who joins at no extra charge; after that, a member who joins mid-period is charged $20 and gets a full monthly allowance. Service accounts are not covered by Go Teams.

See the current list of models. It may change as we test and add new ones.

Workspace privacy settings apply to Go. By default, paid endpoints that train on request data are blocked, and allowed regions are Europe & United States. Workspaces without saved privacy settings also allow Global for requests from China.

In Settings › Privacy, an owner or admin can enable:

  • DeepSeek V4.1 Flash and V4 Pro: select Global under Regions.
  • LongCat 2.5: select Global under Regions.
  • Muse Spark 1.3 Contributor and Muse Spark 1.2 Contributor: turn on Allow paid endpoints that train on request data.

See Privacy for each model’s training and retention terms.

Owners and admins can pay for Go, including renewals and upgrades, from the workspace’s credit. Choose Credit as the payment method when you subscribe, or later in Manage subscription. This is separate from Extra Usage, which pays for usage past your limits.

  • The full price comes from your credit balance. If the balance is short, you can add credit first.
  • Renewals take the price from your credit when the period ends. If there isn’t enough, Go stays active while the renewal retries 1, 3, and 7 days after the period ends, and again as soon as enough credit is added. If the last retry fails, Go ends.
  • Workspaces on invoiced billing can’t pay for Go with credit.
  • A refund of a Go payment made with credit goes back to your credit and ends that Go period.

OpenCode Go is designed for OpenCode and other coding agents that produce similar types of requests. Traffic is monitored for abuse that degrades the experience for other users.

Your client should:

  1. Send typical coding agent traffic
  2. Identify itself with its own user agent, such as my-coding-agent/1.0, rather than a generic SDK or HTTP-library name.
  3. Send a stable session ID in x-opencode-session for each conversation so we can optimize routing and prompt caching.

Decision and embedding requests don’t need x-opencode-session, so any client, such as a RAG library, a memory provider or a script, can call them with your Go key.

See Harnesses to set up Go in Claude Code, Codex, Hermes, Pi, and Raycast.

Besides OpenCode, the following clients have been validated to work properly with OpenCode Go, although we do not guarantee that they will continue to work in the future.

The paid models you use on Go share one allowance. On Go Teams, each member has their own allowance across models.

  • The 5-hour limit is 20% of your monthly allowance. The window starts when you first use your allowance and lasts five hours.
  • The weekly limit is 50% of your monthly allowance. It resets on Monday at 00:00 UTC.
  • Your monthly allowance resets when a new paid period starts.

Switching models draws from the same allowance. If you reach a limit, you can use a free model or turn on Extra Usage to use your Console credits until you have allowance available again.

Each model’s monthly limit shows how much usage your plan covers if you use only that model over the month. For example, $1.50 of usage on a model with a $15 limit uses 10% of your monthly allowance. So does $6 on a model with a $60 limit. Go responses report how much each request counted in opencode.allowance.

Token prices are per 1M tokens and are the same for Go, Go Plus, and Go Teams.

Decision and embedding models come at list price, with no Go discount: their monthly limit matches what you pay, $10 on Go and $40 on Go Plus. On Go Teams it’s $15, $5 higher than Go as on every model.

Beam Free: Free for a limited time. Not available in Angola, Belarus, Cambodia, China (including Hong Kong and Macau), Cuba, the Democratic Republic of the Congo, Equatorial Guinea, Iran, Iraq, Laos, Libya, Nigeria, North Korea, Russia, Syria, Turkmenistan, Uzbekistan, Venezuela, or the Crimea, Donetsk, and Luhansk regions of Ukraine.

Gemini 4 Argon: The 50% discount applies through November 30, 2026.

HungerG Free: Free for a limited time.

Console’s Go page estimates requests per 5 hours for each model, based on typical Go usage patterns. Actual usage varies.

You can track your current usage in the console, or with the usage endpoint.

Tip
If you reach the usage limit, you can continue using the free models.

Usage limits may change as we learn from early usage and feedback.

If you also have credits in your Console balance, you can turn on Extra Usage on the Go page. When enabled, Go will fall back to your pay-as-you-go balance after you’ve reached your usage limits instead of blocking requests.

Go Teams starts with Extra Usage on, and you can turn it off or on again on the Go page.

With Go, the included monthly usage varies by model.

For most models, we make this work through bulk discounts and reserved GPU capacity. We then pass those savings on to you as higher monthly usage.

For some models, we haven’t had the opportunity to negotiate a discount or host them at a lower cost, either because the model is new or because their public pricing is already discounted.

For these models, you still get a little more than if you paid the model providers directly; this is why their included monthly usage is lower.

You can also access Go models through the API at https://opencode.ai/inference/go. See each model’s ID, path and AI SDK package. Each model is listed with its native API, but every text model also works through the other APIs, which Console translates. See Base URLs for the full set.

These AI SDK packages are for applications calling Go directly. To use Go in OpenCode, connect with /connect and set the model using the format opencode-go/<model-id>:

opencode.jsonc
{
  "$schema": "https://opencode.ai/config.json",
  "model": "opencode-go/kimi-k3",
}

Fetch the full list of available models and their metadata from the models endpoint:

Terminal
curl https://opencode.ai/inference/go/v1/models

See the model list API for every field in the response, including Go’s monthly usage limits.

Decision models answer typed questions about a state instead of generating text. Send the same System One request as on Console models to https://opencode.ai/inference/go/systemone/v1/systemone with your Go key:

Terminal
curl https://opencode.ai/inference/go/systemone/v1/systemone \
  -H "Authorization: Bearer $OPENCODE_API_KEY" \
  -H "Content-Type: application/json" \
  -d '{
    "model": "jev-1.14",
    "state": "My payments have failed for three days and I am losing sales.",
    "questions": {
      "is_urgent": {
        "type": "noul",
        "instructions": "Does this request require urgent attention?"
      }
    }
  }'

Check how much of your usage limits you’ve used, for example to show them in your own client:

Terminal
curl https://opencode.ai/inference/go/v1/usage \
  -H "Authorization: Bearer <token>"

Use the same key you use for Go. On Go and Go Plus, that is the subscriber’s personal API key or a service account key. On Go Teams, it is any member’s personal API key.

The response has one meter for each limit: rolling for the 5-hour limit, weekly, and monthly.

JSON
{
  "usage": {
    "rolling": { "status": "ok", "percent": 30, "resetsAt": "2026-11-21T15:00:00.000Z" },
    "weekly": { "status": "rate-limited", "percent": 100, "resetsAt": "2026-11-23T00:00:00.000Z" },
    "monthly": { "status": "ok", "percent": 50, "resetsAt": "2026-12-01T00:00:00.000Z" }
  }
}
FieldMeaning
statusok, or rate-limited once the limit is used up.
percentHow much of the limit is used, as a whole number from 0 to 100.
resetsAtWhen the limit resets, in ISO 8601. Before the 5-hour window starts, rolling resets five hours from now.

Requests without a valid key return HTTP 401. Keys Go doesn’t cover return HTTP 403 with a message that explains why, for example when the workspace has no active Go subscription.

JSON
{ "type": "error", "error": { "type": "EntitlementError", "message": "OpenCode Go subscription required." } }

Open models have gotten really good. They now reach performance close to proprietary models for coding tasks. Because many providers can serve them competitively, they are usually far cheaper.

However, getting reliable, low latency access to them can be difficult. Providers vary in quality and availability.

Tip
We tested a select group of models and providers that work well with OpenCode.

To fix this, we did a couple of things:

  1. We tested a select group of open models and talked to their teams about how to best run them.
  2. We worked with a few providers to make sure these were being served correctly.
  3. We benchmarked the combination of the model/provider and came up with a list that we feel good recommending.

Go, Go Plus, and Go Teams all provide access to these models; choose the plan that fits how much you use them, or Go Teams to cover your whole team.

We created OpenCode Go to:

  1. Make AI coding accessible to more people with a low cost subscription.
  2. Provide reliable access to the best open coding models.
  3. Curate models that are tested and benchmarked for coding agent use.
  4. Have no lock-in by allowing you to use any other provider with OpenCode as well.
