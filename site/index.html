Methodology
How scores are calculated across 13 current zero-gap dimensions, with explicit cost construction and coverage provenance.

Scoring Formula

Score = ((n − rank) / (n − 1)) × 100

For each of 12 dimensions, all 21 models are ranked 1–21 (best to worst). The formula maps rank 1 → 100 pts and rank 21 → 0 pts, with tied ranks receiving the average of their tied positions. v1.4.0 has no missing-data cells and therefore uses no neutral-fill values.

The final ValueRank score is a weighted sum across all 12 dimensions. Cost weight: 25% (slider-adjustable). Hallucination rate remains a primary reliability factor, and cost is constructed from normalized AA eval cost plus normalized DeepSWE average cost before rank-normalization.

Key Design Choices

Rank-based (not raw-score-based): Prevents any single benchmark from dominating due to scale differences. GPQA percentages, DeepSWE pass rates, and token-per-second speed figures are not directly comparable on raw values; ranks are.
Zero-gap benchmark rule: If even one of the 21-model cohort is genuinely missing from a benchmark, that benchmark is excluded from the current primary score. The official-source owner is checked first, then current secondary implementations are checked before a benchmark is ruled out.
SEAL CI band (±2–3 pts): Due to benchmark variance, scores within 2–3 pts should be treated as statistically tied. Rankings within this band are effectively co-ranked.
Quality sub-score: The 68% non-cost portion, computed as the weighted sum of all 11 non-cost dimensions. Represents pure capability ranking without cost penalty.

FAQ
Frequently Asked Questions
Common questions about how ValueRank works, what the scores mean, and how to interpret results.
Each model's score is a weighted average of 13 normalized dimension scores. Each dimension score is calculated as ((n − rank) / (n − 1)) × 100, where n = 21 for every retained v1.4.0 dimension. A score of 100 = top of pool and 0 = bottom of pool. There are no neutral missing-data cells in v1.4.0. For AA-Omniscience Hallucination Rate, lower raw hallucination rate is ranked better. For Cost, the raw input is a composite of normalized AA eval cost plus normalized DeepSWE average cost per task.
ValueRank is designed for production software engineering teams, not academic benchmarking. At scale, a 10× cost difference between similarly capable models is a real operational constraint, but one pricing surface is not enough. The 25% cost weight therefore now combines Artificial Analysis eval cost with DeepSWE average cost per task into a single cost-efficiency term. You can drag the cost weight slider in the Rankings section to 0% to see a pure quality ranking if budget is no constraint.
In historical ValueRank versions, ⊘ meant a genuine benchmark data gap. In v1.4.0, there are no ⊘ cells because any benchmark without full cohort coverage is excluded entirely. The Coverage section should therefore show ✓ Full for every ranked model.
The overall Score includes all 12 retained dimensions with cost at 25% (slider default) and Speed is shown separately when an AA page publishes a numeric value. The Quality Score is recalculated using only the 11 non-cost dimensions (renormalized to 100%). It answers: "which model is best at actual tasks?" independent of composite cost. The Rankings table shows both, and the Quality Rank column lets you sort by quality alone.
The composite cost term values models that stay efficient on both public pricing surfaces. That is why Gemini 3.8 Flash is overall #1 in v1.4.0, while Claude Fable 5 is the current quality leader in the cohort (Quality #1) but falls to overall #3 because its composite cost is the highest in the cohort.
The Pareto frontier contains models that no other single model beats on both quality and cost simultaneously. In v1.2 the frontier is 7 models: Grok 4.5, Kimi K3, Muse Spark 1.1, GPT-5.6 Terra, GPT-5.6 Sol, Claude Opus 5, and Gemini 3.1 Pro. Every other ranked model is dominated by at least one frontier model on both dimensions.
