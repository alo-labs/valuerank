Methodology

How ValueRank scores a model

A model's score is its value: cost per Intelligence Index point carries a fixed 25% of the score and benchmark quality the other 75%. The quality part is a weighted average of 0–100 dimension scores. If a quality dimension has no eligible value for a model it is left out and the remaining quality weights are scaled up, so a gap never counts as zero and never gets filled in.

Model inclusion

Frontier rule. Every model family on the Pareto frontier that Artificial Analysis (AA) renders on its Intelligence Index versus total cost to run chart is ranked, with the exact configuration AA selects on that line. Frontier membership is AA's rendered line as observed; ValueRank never recomputes it from rounded values.

Near-frontier rule. A plotted configuration is near-frontier when its public (rounded integer) Intelligence Index is within 3 points of the rendered frontier's Index at its cost, meaning the highest Index among rendered-frontier points that cost no more. Every near-frontier family is ranked (its selected configuration may differ from the near configuration) or is explicitly deferred by a dated, reasoned roster decision. The cost ratio to the frontier is reported for information only.

Evidence grades

A model's grade is the weakest grade among the values inside its score.

  • Owner-measured Measured by the benchmark owner or in a controlled account measurement.
  • Provider claim Published by the model provider for the exact benchmark version and evaluated variant.
  • Modelled Owner estimate or projection anchored to a measurement.
  • Assumption Explicitly authorised assumption with no measurement.
  • Missing No eligible value; excluded from the score and the score is renormalised.

Limitations

  • Rank and cost scores are relative to the ranked cohort and change when the cohort changes.
  • Public benchmark displays are rounded, so small differences between models are not meaningful.
  • Subscription value multiples are projections or assumptions, not guarantees of what a plan delivers.
  • Results are never transferred between model versions or reasoning efforts; missing coverage lowers confidence.
  • Cost per Index point assumes the Artificial Analysis token mix, not your own workload.

Questions

How is the overall score calculated?

Cost carries a fixed 25% of the score and quality the rest. The cost score compares USD per Intelligence Index point on a log scale, so the cheapest model scores 100, the dearest 0, and equal cost ratios count equally. Each quality dimension is rank-normalised to 0-100 across the ranked cohort, multiplied by its weight and divided by the quality weights that are available for that model.

What happens when a benchmark has no result for a model?

The dimension is dropped for that model and the remaining quality weights are renormalised; cost keeps its fixed share. Nothing is filled in, and the gap is shown on the model page.

What is the difference between the API ranking and the Plan ranking?

Both rank on token efficiency: what it costs to run the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index, per Index point. The API ranking prices the run at API rates. The Plan ranking also divides by the best eligible subscription Value Multiple, the API-priced usage a plan delivers per dollar of fee.

What do the evidence grades mean?

Measured values come from the benchmark owner or a controlled account measurement. Claims come from the model provider, estimates are owner estimates or user-reported projections, and assumptions are explicitly authorised stand-ins.

Why is a model not ranked?

Ranked models need an exact-variant DeepSWE and Bug Hunt result or a required place on the Artificial Analysis frontier. Unranked models still have pages with every value we hold.
